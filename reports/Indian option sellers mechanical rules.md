# Indian Option Sellers Hide Their Rules, Repackage Global Ones

Almost nobody in India publishes verifiable mechanical rules for systematic index-options selling, and what is published is a thin, unaudited, largely imported heuristic set. This research found **no dedicated, named SEBI-registered PMS or AIF index-options-income product**, no prop or quant firm that discloses anything about an options book, no educator with a verifiable track record, and **no published Indian ML-gated options-selling algorithm** comparable to this backtester's per-regime LightGBM entry model. The concrete rules that do exist come from algo platforms and retail educators: the 9:20 AM ATM short straddle with a 35-40% per-leg stop and a 3:10-3:15 PM exit (Marketcalls, Streak), uTrade's India VIX operating bands (12-30 and 15-30) with Friday and event-day exclusions, and an educator consensus of a 2x-credit stop, roughly 50% profit booking, and roll-the-untested-leg or convert-to-defined-risk adjustments. None of it is SEBI-audited. Every platform-page claim here reaches us only through search-snippet paraphrase because direct fetches of marketcalls.in, algotest.in, utradealgos.com, zerodha.com and tradingqna.com failed with DNS errors. The consensus playbook looks like a repackaged tastytrade/Option Alpha-style canon rather than an India-specific validated edge, and Marketcalls itself reports that the 9:20 straddle's edge faded, especially in 2023. Given this repo's own chain-engine verdict (weekly premium selling NOT SUPPORTED on real NSE settlement prices, expectancy -177/-238/-348 Rs/lot at 1.0x/1.5x/2.0x slippage), the useful output is a short list of retail heuristics worth falsifying, not adopting. Section 4 ranks them.

## The honest answer to "who is doing this in India" is mostly negative

**Regulated vehicles.** Searches turned up no PMS or AIF with "options income," "iron condor," or "covered call" in its product name or stated strategy. The SEBI-registered vehicles that touch options use them as a sleeve inside long-short or hedging mandates. Nuvama's EDGE is a Category III long-short AIF investing in cash equities plus stock and index futures and options, with three sub-strategies whose hedging policies are not public ([PMS Bazaar](https://pmsbazaar.com/News/Edelweiss-Global-Wealth-Management-Ltd/Edelweiss_launches_new_long-short_AIF)). Avendus runs a quant Market Neutral Fund and an Absolute Return Fund that use equity derivatives for lower volatility, described only at the level of objectives ([Avendus](https://avendus.com/india/newsroom/avendus-alternate-strategies-announces-the-launch-of-market-neutral-fund)). True Beacon One is roughly 60% long equity and 40% long-short derivatives book, again with no strike, structure or stop disclosure ([PMS AIF World](https://www.pmsaifworld.com/?p=29157)). SEBI's own April 2025 PMS statistics, as relayed by search, put "equity derivatives" at about **₹214 crore against roughly ₹37 lakh crore** of PMS assets ([SEBI](https://www.sebi.gov.in/statistics/assets-managed/archive-month/apr-2025.html)). That figure is provisional because the page was not re-fetched. It still implies that a large index-options-selling book does not sit under the PMS wrapper, consistent with PMS rules that limit derivatives mainly to hedging and rebalancing. The closest named matches are a **Capitalmind "StratOptions"/"Uncle Theta"** monthly-income approach (stated target 2-3% a month, running since December 2015, theta-based; its current registration and whether it is still live could not be confirmed) ([Capitalmind](https://premium.capitalmind.in/tag/nifty-options)), and **Wright Research's "Theta Decay Intraday"**, which sells naked and/or hedged weekly options with daily rebalancing at a ₹2 lakh minimum. That ticket is far below the ₹50 lakh PMS floor, so it is almost certainly an RA/IA model portfolio rather than a PMS ([Wright Research](https://www.wrightresearch.in/portfolio/option-selling/download)). Neither discloses a stop, DTE or drawdown number. Mutual funds can only write single-stock covered calls, capped at 15% of the equity holding's market value, so no mutual fund runs index-level income ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/article/markets/sebi-allows-mutual-funds-to-write-call-options-under-certain-conditions-119011700021_1.html)). No SEBI order or press report found ties an options-selling PMS/AIF to investor losses. That is more consistent with tiny regulated scale than with safety.

**Prop and quant firms.** iRage, QuadEye, Graviton, AlphaGrep, Futures First and others are profiled only for business model, funding or headcount, never for options mechanics ([Euromoney](https://www.euromoney.com/article/3bkyu7lits6c480gkco8cgwgk/indias-best-broker-2026-irage/); [tradermath QuadEye](https://www.tradermath.org/firms/quadeye); [Bloomberg Law](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/private-equity/high-speed-tradings-homegrown-india-unicorn-is-going-global)). Tower Research India and Edelweiss's prop desk produced nothing citable. The only scale datapoint is Bloomberg's report that algorithmic and prop traders collectively made about **$7 billion** of gross profit from Indian index futures and options in the year to March 2024 ([BNN Bloomberg](https://bnnbloomberg.ca/business/international/2024/10/03/why-indias-giant-options-market-poses-a-danger-to-financial-stability)). The profit is real, but it says nothing about which strategies earned it, and it is likely dominated by market-making rather than directional premium selling. Firms are not required to disclose, and SEBI's retail-algo framework now pushes black-box strategy sellers into Research Analyst registration, which discourages publishing logic ([AlgoTest](https://algotest.in/blog/algo-trading-india.md)).

**Educators.** P R Sundar, the most prominent, was barred for a year in 2023 for unregistered advisory activity, and no primary source for his numeric rules was found. Critics allege selective profit screenshots ([Storyboard18](https://www.storyboard18.com/brand-makers/who-is-pr-sundar-mercedes-and-jaguar-owning-options-trader-living-in-a-rs-30-crore-penthouse-3659.htm); [TradingQnA](https://tradingqna.com/t/trading-profit-shown-in-youtube-and-various-contradictory-aspects-found/120381/20)). No rule sets could be located for Vivek Bajaj, Ankit Gala or SpiderSoftwareIndia, so treat them as unlocated rather than nonexistent. Rachana Ranade sells an index option-selling webinar with a "5-Year Back tested Data" claim, but the methodology, win rate and drawdown are unpublished ([Rachana Ranade](https://www.rachanaranade.com/courses/Option-Selling-Strategy-with-Discipline--Structure-Live-Webinar-Batch-3-69e5ede02f7f09864979ac70)). Even a Udemy seller concedes that without adjustment "75% of strangles end in a loss" ([Udemy](https://www.udemy.com/course/nifty-option-selling-monthly-and-weekly/)), a marketing statistic with no method behind it. The wider context is SEBI's finding that over 91% of individual F&O traders lost money in FY25 ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/markets/news/net-losses-of-traders-in-fo-widens-in-fy25-sebi-study-125070701221_1.html)).

**Open code and academia.** GitHub has data-plumbing repos and a trivial 9:20 straddle script, but nothing combining VIX routing with an ML win-probability gate ([GitHub nifty50 topic](https://github.com/topics/nifty50)). The nearest methodological analogue is a non-Indian S&P 500 short-straddle classifier: initial results showed no significant gain over "trade always," and a later gradient-boosted model reportedly did ([AI Finance Frontier](https://theaifinancefrontier.beehiiv.com/p/machine-learning-short-straddles-sp500)). No QuantInsti EPAT capstone on Nifty option selling surfaced, and the Indian academic literature is dominated by pricing-efficiency and smile studies, not selling backtests ([IIMA, Varma](https://web.iima.ac.in/publications/data/2002-04-01JRVarma.pdf)). The one selling-relevant academic result, a Vaasa thesis on Nifty straddles, found negative Sharpe ratios for both long and short ([Vaasa](https://osuva.uwasa.fi/handle/11111/19466)). That thesis was read only at abstract level.

Three caveats follow. First, the sources disagree on small numbers: Marketcalls gives a 35% stop on one page and 40% on another, and the exit is 3:10 PM on one and 3:15 PM in Streak, so none of these are canonical parameters. Second, the sources disagree on the sign of the VIX filter: uTrade's bands floor at 12-15 and run up to 30, while the unattributed TradingView note favors VIX below 13-14. An ablation by VIX band can therefore settle a real disagreement. Third, Streak's forum representative says its backtest ignores margin and recommends judging on absolute P&L after brokerage, tax and slippage, so any Streak percentage-return figure is unreliable ([TradingQnA](https://tradingqna.com/t/is-nifty-income-short-straddle-strategy-on-streak-really-profitable-see-details/122634)). Quantsapp, TradeSmart and QuantInsti yielded no mechanical rule set at all. For Quantsapp that is a likely true absence, since its algos appear to be gated inside the product; for TradeSmart and QuantInsti it is a search-coverage gap.

## The consensus playbook is imported, and its flagship edge has already faded

The recurring Indian retail rule cluster is a 1x-2x-credit stop, about 50% profit booking, a premium-doubling or fixed-loss trigger to adjust, and then roll-untested, hedge or convert-to-defined-risk. It is substantively the same as the US options-education canon. The notes could not attribute any numeric rule to a named Indian educator, and the sources cite none of each other. Entri, Equities India, OneTradeJournal and course blurbs converge independently, but the convergence is on global conventions. For comparison, tastylive's own research manages iron condors and flies around 25-50% of max profit ([tastylive](https://www.tastylive.com/shows/market-measures/episodes/iron-flies-straddle-profit-targets-12-06-2016)). The earlier research for this project showed that those same conventions are variance-reduction and capital-recycling devices, not reliably expectancy-maximizing: the 2016 SPY iron-condor study lifted win rate from 53% to 62% with 50% management while average P/L dipped slightly ([tastylive](https://www.tastylive.com/shows/options-jive/episodes/iv-rank-strategy-selection-01-06-2017)). Indian delta-based strike selection was entirely absent from the sources. India-specific content is limited to ATM-straddle or fixed-point distances, fixed-clock entries, and the exchange structure: a single Nifty weekly since November 2024 and the Thursday-to-Tuesday expiry cutover.

The 9:20 straddle is the best-documented Indian rule, and Marketcalls says it has stopped working, especially in 2023. That is a self-assessment by a platform with no audit, so it cannot be verified, but it fits the structural story. Once a strategy is widely templated into no-code builders, everyone sells the same strikes at the same minute, and the open is the widest-spread, most gap-sensitive part of the session. The suggested move to 10:30 AM points in the same direction as this backtester's 11:00-13:00 window and its +2.47% CAGR / MaxDD 8.9% to 4.7% mid-session finding. That is directional corroboration of the existing design, not new evidence for it. The repo's own result is on a mid-session proxy (`nifty_open + 0.40*(close-open)`) and Black-Scholes prices, and the NSE archive has no intraday data, so neither claim can be validated intraday here.

The decisive calibration is the repo's chain-engine result. On real NSE bhavcopy settle prices (2019-02 to 2026-08), weekly short premium showed expectancy of **-177/-238/-348 Rs/lot** and a pre-registered NOT SUPPORTED verdict, and the model-priced weekly CAGRs were not evidence of an edge. Nothing in the Indian retail material contradicts that. It supplies no audited, real-price counter-evidence, only heuristics that work for the people selling them in terms of course sales and platform signups.

## What can be learned, and what cannot

Little of the evidence can be used to calibrate parameters. No result in this corpus carries a drawdown, a trade log, or a real-price methodology, so none can serve as a benchmark for `backtest_runs.jsonl`. The evidence can be used for three narrower things. It identifies the hypotheses the Indian practitioner market actually holds, in cheap-to-test form. It exposes where practitioners contradict each other, as with VIX direction, which marks the questions with information value. And it supplies execution-risk warnings worth carrying into any live-signal work: SL-M stop slippage, Streak's roughly one-minute re-entry lag, and stale absolute-rupee stops (uTrade halved its stop and target in April 2024, which suggests parameters get re-tuned to recent regimes).

Several things cannot be learned. There is no evidence about which rules a profitable institutional seller uses. Prop-firm silence is a result, not a search failure, since options edge is among the most guarded IP. Non-disclosure at SEBI level is structural: PMS/AIF disclosure documents state objectives and risk category, not strike rules, and none was read directly. Survivorship bias is a standing risk, as the Bruton case showed in the US (concealed losses of more than $50 million behind a celebrated naked-strangle record ([SEC](https://www.sec.gov/litigation/litreleases/2018/lr24285.htm))), and the Indian screenshot allegations are the same pattern, unproven. The clearest unfilled gaps are direct page reads of the platform sites, the QuantInsti and TradeSmart sites, Kaggle, an exhaustive GitHub text search, SEBI's orders database, and any RA/IA registry check for a systematic Nifty-selling adviser.

## Seven patterns not already in this backtester, ranked for testing

Excluded because the design already has them: mid-session fills, VIX gating and VIX-adaptive exits, defined-risk IC/PCS/BWB structures, the DD kill-switch, the 50% profit target and 100%-of-credit weekly stop (the "double the premium" convention), the expiry-minus-one-day exit (the Substack "close a day before expiry" rule), and the Budget/RBI/FOMC/election/monthly-expiry event blocks in `backtester/production_rules.py`. That also covers convert-to-spread-before-events, since every structure here is already defined-risk. All of the following are tier B or C retail heuristics, to be falsified against the chain engine (`scripts/run_chain_backtest.py`) before any adoption, and the chain engine is EOD-only, with no bid/ask and no intraday stops. None can be promoted on authority.

The cheapest, most informative experiments are ranks 1, 2 and 5. They need only config grids, one feature column and a price-data calculation, and they can reuse `--mode ablation` and the existing chain runner. Ranks 3 and 4 need exit-policy code. If `scripts/log_option_quotes.py` captures intraday quotes (not checked), it is the natural route to accumulating the intraday data that ranks 4 and 6 need.

## Conclusion

The new knowledge here is mainly a map of what is absent. India's regulated wrappers do not house systematic index-options income, prop desks keep their books private, and the retail layer sells a global heuristic set whose single flagship Indian-specific rule is reported by its best-documented publisher to have decayed. That removes a tempting source of outside authority: there is no Indian published edge the backtester should be trying to match, and its own real-price result stands uncontradicted. The most useful signals are the disagreements, namely VIX direction and entry time, because they identify where a controlled experiment has the most to say.

Three practical rules follow. Treat every pattern above as a hypothesis, label results by BS engine versus chain engine, and promote only what survives on real settlement prices at 1.5x-2.0x slippage. Re-run the missing primary-source reads (platform pages, Varsity, QuantInsti, SEBI orders) once fetch access works, because every platform-sourced number here rests on snippet paraphrase and could shift. And expect the likeliest outcome of the ranked tests to be a modest refinement of the existing VIX and exit logic, not a new source of alpha.
