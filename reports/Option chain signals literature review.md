# Option-Chain Signals Cannot Rescue Nifty Selling

**Evidence-quality statement, first.** None of the ten research streams behind this report could read a paper's full text. Only github.com was reachable from the research sandbox; arXiv, SSRN, NBER, RePEc, publisher sites, SEBI and NSE returned DNS failures or proxy 403s. Every figure below therefore comes from abstracts, search summaries or secondary write-ups, with one exception: one researcher read the author's public GitHub replication package for Vilkov (2026) directly ([repo](https://github.com/vilkovgr/0dte-strategies)). Subject to that limit, the answer is this. The literature does not support any option-chain selector that would turn the repo's negative net expectancy (-177/-238/-348 Rs/lot at 1.0x/1.5x/2.0x slippage on real NSE EOD settles) positive. The most consistent finding across the sources is that bid-ask costs and margin consume the short-premium edge. Hold-to-expiry defined-risk variants sit near break-even, with break-even slippage of about 0.44x for the baseline and about 1.42x for hold-to-expiry on the corrected cost model ([BACKTEST_CHANGELOG.md](../BACKTEST_CHANGELOG.md)). The strongest signal evidence needs data the repo does not have (signed order flow, bid/ask, intraday bars). The signals buildable from EOD settle, OI and volume have the weakest evidence, and most proxy for India VIX, ATM IV or days to expiry. The highest-value next steps are therefore to measure real costs with the quote logger and to run the pre-registered structure comparison (spread vs naked vs condor, wing width, hold-to-expiry). Adding signals comes after that. Twelve candidate rules are ranked in section 3, a "do not build" list is in section 4, and a pre-registration protocol limits multiple-testing damage in section 5.

## No paper's full text was read, so every number is a lead, not a fact

All ten streams (structures and costs, return decomposition, hedges, IV-RV timing, skew, demand pressure and dealer gamma, OI and pinning, short-dated and exit rules, term structure and sizing, India and emerging markets) report the same access failure. WebFetch returned DNS errors, and the egress proxy refused CONNECT to the academic hosts. Only WebSearch worked, and it returns model-written summaries of pages rather than the pages. This report tags claims by what was actually read. **Read** means primary text and applies only to the Vilkov replication package (paper annotations, digest, README, KNOWN-ISSUES, output tables); even there the SSRN PDF was not opened and the Git LFS data panels were stale, so nothing was recomputed ([repo](https://github.com/vilkovgr/0dte-strategies)). **Abstract** means the summary of a paper's abstract or listing. **Secondary** means CXO Advisory, Quantpedia, press or citing-paper relays. **Vendor** means promotional or commercially interested sources (tastylive, Option Alpha, OptionMetrics blogs, SqueezeMetrics, SpotGamma, FlashAlpha, Cboe-sponsored index research). Nearly every paper lacks t-statistics, cost tables and robustness checks in what was retrieved.

Independence is also thinner than the citation count suggests. The AQR hedging papers (2011, 2015, 2016, 2019, 2021, 2025) are one research group applying similar CBOE indices, so they are not five confirmations ([AQR Chasing Your Own Tail Revisited](https://www.aqr.com/-/media/AQR/Documents/Insights/White-Papers/AQR-Chasing-Your-Own-Tail-Risk-Revisited.pdf?sc_lang=en)). Evidence on Nifty itself consists of a handful of abstracts, two preprints and a master's thesis.

## The literature agrees on one thing: frictions eat the premium

The structure and hedge evidence points one way. Santa-Clara and Saretto's gross Sharpe ratios rise with moneyness and fall with maturity (a near-maturity 10% OTM strangle at 0.654 against 0.247 for the ATM straddle, units unclear), but a 5% OTM call costs 17.4% of its price round-trip near maturity against 2.7% far from expiry ([UCLA summary](https://www.anderson.ucla.edu/documents/areas/fac/finance/santa_clara_option.pdf), [UCLA option_strategies](https://www.anderson.ucla.edu/documents/areas/fac/finance/option_strategies.pdf)). Margin per premium dollar rises from $3.9 for far-maturity ATM calls to $43.1 for near-maturity 10% OTM calls, and including margin calls turned the average return of a near-maturity 5% OTM strangle from +2.7% to -1.0% per month ([NBER copy](https://conference.nber.org/confer/2005/bfs05/saretto.pdf)). Hong, Sung and Yang reach a similar conclusion: put-writing profits shrink sharply after bid-ask spreads and invite margin calls ([IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/reveco/v55y2018icp295-307.html)). The corner with the best gross Sharpe, near-maturity and far OTM, has the worst cost and margin intensity, which is where a weekly seller trades. Profits that survive costs in this literature come from selection rules, not fixed periodic selling: Constantinides et al. find writing options that violate stochastic-dominance bounds raises utility net of spreads ([NBER w16302](https://www.nber.org/papers/w16302)), and Faias and Santa-Clara report net Sharpe of 0.59-0.82 for an optimized portfolio ([IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/cup/jfinqa/v52y2017i01p277-303_00.html)). Neither is a simple strangle or condor, and neither is Nifty.

### Wings are the economic crux, and no paper tests them with real spreads

The notes searched for a peer-reviewed comparison of put spreads, iron condors or broken-wing butterflies with naked short strikes and found none, stating it as the central gap ([note 02](../research_notes/Option%20chain%20signals%20literature%20review/02_index_option_return_decomposition_wings.md)). What exists is a decomposition debate. Broadie, Chernov and Johannes argue that large put-writing returns are statistically consistent with Black-Scholes or Heston once sampling error is counted, and that ATM straddle returns need a jump-risk premium ([SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=965739)). Bondarenko says no model in his class explains put prices ([IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/wsi/qjfxxx/v04y2014i03ns2010139214500153.html)). Constantinides, Jackwerth and Savov find returns decline in strike-to-price ratio and that only crash factors (market jumps, volatility jumps, liquidity) explain the cross-section ([SSRN](https://www.ssrn.com/abstract=1491469)). Coval and Shumway find zero-beta ATM straddle buyers lost about 3% a week, read as a priced volatility factor ([SSRN](https://www.ssrn.com/abstract=189840)). Taken together, the wing premium is largely crash compensation, so a short backtest cannot tell edge from compensation, and a defined-risk wing both caps the compensated tail and buys the contract these studies show to be expensive. Whether a wing raises or lowers expected return is therefore empirical, not derivable from the literature.

The only rigorous condor-type evidence is Vilkov's 0DTE package, read directly. In a 953-observation table the long iron butterfly/condor had a mean of -0.0073% of spot at mid, -0.0324% after half-spread costs and -0.0374% after an extra 0.5 bp of slippage ([repo](https://github.com/vilkovgr/0dte-strategies)). Sign-flipping to the short side gives a gross edge of roughly +0.007% against costs near 0.030% of spot; this is the researcher's arithmetic, and the notes do not say whether that table was regenerated after the cost-unit fix. Short OTM strangles won in at least 75% of observations with means not distinguishable from zero, and expected shortfall at 1% ran 0.58-1.58% of underlying. Corrected unconditional net Sharpe ratios were -2.67 for the (long) iron butterfly/condor and -0.97 for the strangle/straddle ([repo KNOWN-ISSUES](https://github.com/vilkovgr/0dte-strategies)). The lesson that transfers to a weekly Nifty book is that win rate is the wrong objective: the profile is high win rate, near-zero or negative mean and a fat left tail.

### Hedges for a short-vol book behave as costs, and the pro-hedge case is promotional

Israelov and Klein find the CBOE 95-110 collar earned a Sharpe of 0.30 against 0.47 for the S&P 500, with excess return 3.2% against 7.3% ([ResearchGate listing](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/304610926_Risk_and_Return_of_Equity_Index_Collar_Strategies)). The 2025 buffer-fund review finds selling calls to fund puts still has negative net value ([pm-research](https://www.pm-research.com/content/iijpormgmt/51/10/120)). "Still Not Cheap" argues the volatility risk premium, not the IV level, makes puts cheap or dear, with a secondary figure of 3.4% average VRP positive 88% of the time over 1990-2014 ([SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=2579232), [Alpha Architect](https://alphaarchitect.com/in-calm-markets-should-we-buy-cheap-put-protection/)). "Pathetic Protection" finds statically de-risking beats protective puts on drawdown in most windows, though puts do better in large crashes ([SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=2934538)). The case for small far-OTM put allocations comes from Universa, which sells the product, and its 4,144% Q1-2020 headline is reported as a return on required invested capital, a small fraction of assets managed ([FA Magazine](https://www.fa-mag.com/news/why-one-firm-s-3-612--return-is-drawing-the-ire-of-hedge-funds-72668.html)); CalPERS exited its program on cost grounds ([Institutional Investor](https://www.institutionalinvestor.com/article/b1lf9zw3n8w3gt/Was-CalPERS-Right-to-Unwind-Its-Tail-Risk-Program)). The covered-call decomposition adds that the short-volatility leg carries a Sharpe near 1.0 on about 10% of the risk, while an uncompensated "equity reversal" exposure supplies about 25% of risk with little return ([SSRN 2444999](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/Papers.cfm?abstract_id=2444999)). For a book that is already defined-risk, the wings are the hedge, and a standing tail-put or collar duplicates them.

### Timing on the IV-RV spread is the best-supported idea, and the repo already has it

The most replicated timing direction is to sell when implied volatility is high relative to expected realized volatility. Goyal and Saretto sort single-stock straddles and delta-hedged options on historical minus implied volatility and report large, risk-factor-robust returns ([summary](https://academicnewsletter.sufe.edu.cn/info/357238)); Zhan et al. report single-stock Sharpe above two after costs from firm characteristics that an index lacks ([abstract](https://research.polyu.edu.hk/en/publications/option-return-predictability/)); Bali et al. find option-based characteristics are the strongest predictors in a 265-characteristic machine-learning setup, with out-of-sample R-squared above 2.5% in the working-paper version ([SSRN 3895984](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=3895984)). All three are cross-sectional single-stock results from US data. Index time-series evidence is thin and conflicting. A working paper finds that the sign of the VRP, not the VIX percentile, drives Sharpe gains in variance-swap and VIX-futures trading ([PKU](https://english.phbs.pku.edu.cn/uploadfile/2024/0530/20240530092144610.pdf)). A longer test finds that scaling by a standardized index adds no timing alpha (Sharpe 1.49 unconditional vs 1.36 scaled, paired t of -1.85 over 329 months) ([arXiv 2608.20020](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.20020)). Cheng's VIX premium does not rise with risk, which cuts against sizing up when vol is high ([SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=2495414)). Jones and Wang find that the S&P 500 implied-vol term-structure slope has no predictive power for index straddle returns, although it does for stocks ([PDF](https://msbfile03.usc.edu/digitalmeasures/christoj/intellcont/jones_wang-1.pdf)).

The repo's own premium audit is a sharper constraint than any paper. On weekly cadence the mean implied move was 1.644% against 1.588% realized, implied exceeded realized in 61.6% of weeks, and the ATM straddle's gross edge of +8.2 points per unit had a confidence interval crossing zero. By VIX bucket the gross edge ran -3.8 below 13, +10.2 at 13-16, +25.2 at 16-20, +14.8 at 20-25 and -57.7 above 25. Monthly showed no premium (3.19% implied vs 3.28% realized) ([BACKTEST_CHANGELOG.md](../BACKTEST_CHANGELOG.md)). The Nifty VRP is thin before costs. The existing `vol_risk_premium` and `iv_rv_term_spread` features, the VIX gates and the open VIX-floor-13 arm already cover the literature's main timing idea. The only incremental versions the notes support are a forecast-based expected RV (a HAR-type model, replacing naive trailing HV) and a chain-implied IV in place of India VIX.

### Skew carries little timing information that survives to an index

The stock-level skew effects are large on paper and dated. Steep-smirk stocks underperform flat-smirk ones by 10.9% a year risk-adjusted ([Xing et al. via CXO](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/?p=1798)); expensive calls beat expensive puts by 50 bp a week, a predictability the authors say declined over the sample ([Cremers-Weinbaum](https://ideas.repec.org/a/cup/jfinqa/v45y2010i02p335-367_00.html)); a lowest-minus-highest smile-slope quintile earns 1.9% a month ([Yan](https://academicnewsletter.sufe.edu.cn/info/356921)). An unreviewed 2026 re-estimation finds the one-month smirk coefficient significant in 2015-2019 and insignificant in 2023-2026 ([listing](https://ideas.repec.org/p/arx/papers/2608.26115.html)). For the index, the best item is Atilgan, Bali and Demirtas: the S&P 500 OTM-put-minus-ATM-call IV spread is negatively related to next-day and next-week market returns, the abstract says a strategy beat passive investing after costs, and a secondary summary says significance fades by about one month and gives a 0.02%-0.10% weekly effect per 1% daily spread move ([IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/taf/jnlbes/v33y2015i1p87-101.html), [CXO](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/volatility-effects/predicting-stock-market-returns-with-implied-index-volatilities)). Doran et al. find skew shape signals crashes with significant probability, but the upward-spike prediction is not economically significant, the signal is strongest for short-term OTM puts, and it fades with maturity ([IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/wly/jfutmk/v27y2007i10p921-959.html)). Han shows S&P smile slope and risk-neutral skewness respond to sentiment ([IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/oup/rfinst/v21y2008i1p387-414.html)), so an extreme skew is likelier to be hedging demand than a crash forecast. Kozhan, Neuberger and Schneider put the skew premium at over 40% of the S&P implied-vol slope, yet strategies capturing skew with variance hedged earn an insignificant premium ([IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/oup/rfinst/v26y2013i9p2174-2203.html)). SKEW-index predictive power is null or sign-changing in the blog and thesis material ([R-bloggers](https://www.r-bloggers.com/2019/12/skewed-perceptions/), [SuperMoney](https://www.supermoney.com/encyclopedia/skew-finance)). No source quantifies a skew-conditioned delta shift or compares 25-delta against ATM short puts after bid-ask costs. One secondary lead worth verifying is Tosi and Ziegler: shorting S&P OTM puts one week before expiry gave 8.7% a year at a Sharpe of 2.8 with rare steep drawdowns, relayed only by CXO ([CXO](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/short-selling/when-to-sell-equity-index-put-options/)).

### Term structure and regime sizing rest on VIX-futures samples ending around 2013

Johnson (1996-2013) finds the VIX term-structure slope does not forecast VIX changes but forecasts returns on variance swaps, VIX futures and index straddles; the CXO summary's next-day quintile spreads run 0.29%-1.81% across 18 combinations, and a low slope favors the long-variance side, which is a caution for sellers (the researcher's reading, not spelled out in the digest) ([CXO](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/volatility-effects/vix-term-structure-slope-and-variance-asset-future-returns/)). That sits against Jones and Wang's index null above. A 2025 working paper titled "The Volatility Edge" reports a Sharpe near 1 after costs from a VIX/VIX3M-plus-expected-VRP rule on VIX ETNs ([SFI](https://www.sfi.ch/de/publications/n-25-91-the-volatility-edge-a-dual-approach-for-vix-etns-trading)); a QuantConnect reimplementation gets 0.729 ([QuantConnect](https://www.quantconnect.com/research/21143/harvesting-the-volatility-risk-premium-with-a-dual-vix-signal/)), and another community backtest of a contango/backwardation switch shows Sharpe -0.153 with a 94.8% drawdown ([QuantConnect](https://quantconnect.com/terminal/quantpediaBacktestResult/embedded_backtest_8c8d6b135753cee8c2f4f7f540553b2a.html)). Static short-vol via VIX ETPs had Sharpe below 0.5 over 2006-2017 ([Risk.net](https://www.risk.net/journal-of-investment-strategies/5460971/statistics-of-vix-futures-and-their-applications-to-trading-volatility-exchange-traded-products)). On sizing, Wysocki's Kelly/VIX/hybrid put-writing study on 0-5 DTE SPXW options claims the hybrid balances return with drawdown control, but only the abstract was reached, the cell-level numbers in snippets are garbled and the grid is large, so the best cell likely reflects selection ([arXiv 2508.16598](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.16598)). Vol-of-vol and VIX-level results conflict: higher VVIX predicted worse hedged option returns in 2006-2013 ([CXO](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/volatility-effects/expected-volatility-of-stock-market-volatility-as-a-predictor/)) but showed minimal predictive value in a 2009-2019 SVXY test ([CXO](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/volatility-effects/identifying-vxxxiv-tendencies)). Sizing rules change drawdown, not expectancy, and none of this was tested net of costs on index options.

### Demand pressure and dealer gamma are real in theory and unbuildable from EOD data

Bollen and Whaley tie implied-vol changes to net buying pressure, signed from trade classification ([SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=319261)); Garleanu, Pedersen and Poteshman use dealer and end-user positions to explain why index options are expensive ([NBER](https://www.nber.org/papers/w11843)); Pan and Poteshman's put-call ratio uses buyer-initiated opening volume and predicts single-stock returns by more than 40 bp next day and more than 1% next week ([SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=622869)); Chen, Joslin and Ni use public net buy-to-open of deep OTM S&P puts ([NBER](https://www.nber.org/papers/w25573)). All need trade direction and open/close flags that the NSE archive lacks. A KOSPI200 study reports that the Bollen-Whaley daily result does not hold there ([KAIST](https://dspace.kaist.ac.kr/handle/10203/5421)), and a later paper reports a flipped sign relative to Chen-Joslin-Ni in recent data ([Terstegge](https://julianterstegge.com/assets/papers/intermediary_option_pricing.pdf)). Dealer-gamma papers agree on direction (short-gamma dealers amplify moves, long-gamma dampen): Baltussen et al. find intraday momentum into the last 30 minutes across 60+ futures ([SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=3760365)), and Barbon and Buraschi tie gamma imbalances to intraday momentum and reversal ([SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=3725454)). Where dealer positions are observed from trade data, the aggregate effect on index volatility looks small and damping, per the Cboe-hosted Amaya et al. paper (exchange-hosted, with an interest in 0DTE) ([Cboe PDF](https://cdn.cboe.com/resources/education/research_publications/gammasqueezes.pdf)). The one independent public-OI GEX backtest found (SPY, 2018-2026, n=1,972, vendor-published) says GEX "goes quiet" once ATM IV is controlled ([FlashAlpha](https://flashalpha.com/articles/gex-dex-vex-chex-8-year-backtest-spy-vix-control)). Dealer sign on NSE is unknown. The notes' suggestion that Indian retail is net long options and prop desks net short is an unsourced inference, though SEBI's data are consistent with retail concentration in weekly index options.

### Pinning, max pain and put-call ratios have the weakest chain-based support

Pinning evidence is strongest for US single stocks (closing prices cluster at strikes, shifting returns by at least 16.5 bp per expiry date, 1996-2002) ([HKBU record](https://scholars.hkbu.edu.hk/en/publications/stock-price-clustering-on-option-expiration-dates-3/)) and S&P futures (pulled toward the ATM strike with "anti-cross-pinning" before index option expiry; at least $115 million notional per expiry day) ([SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=1664261)). The hedging story requires dealers net long options ([Zhang-Chen-Cai](https://www.wpunj.edu/Weekly%20Options%20on%20Stock%20Pinning%20upto%20page%208.pdf)), and it implies a pull toward the high-OI strike, not repulsion from it. An unreviewed 2016-2025 SPY study finds no pinning, with one significant result: days with the highest ATM open interest had 16% wider daily ranges ([SSRN 6564078](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=6564078)). The only rigorous max-pain test is a US single-stock cross-section whose effect comes from reversals in small, illiquid stocks ([SSRN 4140487](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4140487)); the "60-70% accuracy" claim for Nifty is unsourced vendor text ([Strike.money](https://www.strike.money/options/max-pain-options)), and broker pages show different max-pain levels for the same expiry (22,850 vs 22,600), so any test needs time-stamped snapshots. For put-call ratios, the signal that works needs signed opening volume. A 2014 study reports unsigned ratios also have some predictive power ([JBF](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S037842661400106X)), a Taiwan study found the public ratio insignificant while the non-public version carried information ([ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0927538X25000241)), and no source tests PCR against subsequent index volatility. The same Taiwan paper's title claims PCR improves option writing, with put-writing Sharpe moving from 0.55 to 0.86 when writing only at a low put-call ratio (summary only).

### Short-dated options: costs scale with leg count, and exit-rule evidence is promotional

The 0DTE literature is young and mostly working papers. Beckmeyer, Branger and Gayda report retail losses averaging $241,000 a day (about $350,000 after daily expiries began), with versions disagreeing on the totals and the cost share ([FoFI abstract](https://wp.lancs.ac.uk/fofi2024/files/2024/04/FoFI-2024-146-Leander-Gayda.pdf)). Almeida, Freire and Hizmeri find a day-level VRP up to four times the monthly one ([FMA](https://www.fma.org/assets/docs/Derivatives2025/Almeida.pdf)); Vilkov's median same-day VRP is about 0.0011% of spot at 10:00 ET, and costs swallow it ([repo](https://github.com/vilkovgr/0dte-strategies)). The definitions differ, so both can hold. Dew-Becker et al. find short-horizon variance-swap returns much lower than long-horizon ones and date a decline in S&P option risk premia to about 2010-2012 ([JFE paper](https://stefanogiglio.org/papers/dew-becker-giglio-le-rodriguez-jfe-2017.pdf), [working paper](https://www.dew-becker.org/documents/option_decline.pdf)). Cboe's weekly put-write index collected 39.3% gross premium a year against 24.1% for the monthly one in 2006-2015, yet over the last ten years the Sharpe ratios were 0.50 and 0.52 before any trading costs ([Indexology](https://www.indexologyblog.com/2016/02/18/paper-by-professor-bondarenko-has-intriguing-new-analysis-of-put-and-wput-indexes/)). On exits, no peer-reviewed study tests stops or profit targets on short index-option spreads with bid-ask costs. Kaminski and Lo show simple stops lower expected return under a random walk and help only with momentum ([MIT](https://dspace.mit.edu/handle/1721.1/114876)); the tastylive and Option Alpha studies say managing winners raises P&L per day while hold-to-expiry raises P&L per trade, so the metric decides the verdict ([tastylive](https://www.tastylive.com/news-insights/Do-Smaller-Losses-Lead-to-Better-Performance)). Vilkov's package tests hold-to-close only.

### India and emerging markets: all the net-of-cost evidence is negative

Indian evidence is mostly abstracts. Varma found Nifty volatility under-priced and implied tails thin in June 2001-February 2002, a sample that is a new-market artifact until retested ([IIMA](https://www.iima.ac.in/publication/mispricing-volatility-theindian-index-options-market)). Smile studies report a U-shape, a smirk at 30 days and wing IV attributed to a liquidity premium ([Shaikh/Emerald](https://www.doi.org/10.1108/JIBR-12-2013-0103), [Management Dynamics](https://managementdynamics.researchcommons.org/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1205&context=journal)). Vega-weighted order imbalance in Nifty options moves changes in the VRP ([Chakrabarti and Kumar](https://sites.uwasa.fi/mfjuva/?p=2597)), and an SSRN preprint reports a positive VRP on 74.9% of days, a hit rate that says nothing about the mean after tail losses ([SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=6530119)). On profitability, a University of Vaasa thesis reports negative Sharpe ratios for both long and short Nifty straddles ([PDF](https://osuva.uwasa.fi/server/api/core/bitstreams/8a34262d-fdad-4af1-8f84-34f088e86a3a/content)). Pillai's preprint covers 119 monthly cycles, January 2015-April 2025, with 0.05% sell-side STT, 0.03% brokerage per leg and 0.5% slippage. Every strategy was negative net; the least bad was a 0.94-moneyness put-write at about -0.9% a year (Sharpe -0.37), and straddle variants lost roughly 44-45% a year ([SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=6876580)). The contrary item is a 2007-2019 monthly study where short strangles beat long ones, with cost treatment unknown ([Risk.net](https://www.risk.net/journal-of-investment-strategies/7721271/strangle-to-resuscitate-evidence-from-india)). KOSPI200 shows no ATM volatility premium and negative delta-hedged gains for OTM options ([Park](https://www.emerald.com/jdqs/article/17/2/67/205260/The-Volatility-Risk-Premium-in-the-KOSPI200-Index)); Chinese SSE 50 options show a positive VRP ([Wiley](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1155/2022/6813797)). The only Indian spread figure is 1.25% for Nifty options, cited secondhand, with a back-of-envelope estimate that spreads were 48% of retail index-option losses ([Alberta PDF](https://www.ualberta.ca/en/finance-department/media-library/documents/vikas-agarwal.pdf)). SEBI's FY25 study put 91% of individual F&O traders in net loss, with losses concentrated in weekly index options ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/markets/news/net-losses-of-traders-in-fo-widens-in-fy25-sebi-study-125070701221_1.html)); the FY26 report cited in the notes has 44% of "majorly options sellers" as loss-makers but the group net negative ([SEBI PDF](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/attachdocs/aug-2026/1787233601328.pdf)). SEBI covers all retail behavior, not disciplined defined-risk sellers.

**Why this order.** R1 and R2 test the quantity every other rule is measured against: whether any structure clears its break-even slippage. The repo's cost decomposition shows how little room signals have. On the earlier (pre-correction) cost model at 30 lots and 1.0x, gross edge was +54 Rs/lot against about -117 entry and -121 exit slippage; on the corrected model at 10 lots the baseline earned +123 at zero slippage and -133 at 1.0x ([BACKTEST_CHANGELOG.md](../BACKTEST_CHANGELOG.md)). A selector must roughly double the gross edge to reach break-even at 1.0x. The literature offers no signal of that size on an index net of costs; its largest numbers (Sharpe above two, 10.9% a year) are single-stock cross-sections, and the largest index timing claim, Vilkov's, was retracted after a cost-unit bug. R3 is cheap and informs R2's asymmetric arms. R4 to R6 reuse data and code that already exist. R7 to R10 start as no-trade predictive regressions because that is the cheapest way to retire them. R11 and R12 only trim drawdown, and the standard set for this project is that overlays which only reduce drawdown are not an edge.

## Measure costs first, then compare structures, and keep the signal list short

**Order of work.** Step 0 costs no engineering: obtain the eleven priority PDFs above and re-extract the figures in the checklist, especially the Santa-Clara and Saretto cost and margin tables, the Vilkov short-side net results, and the Jena and Taiwan PCR papers. Step 1 is R1. The quote logger already targets the weekly structure legs, ATM straddle and plus-or-minus two strike neighbours during 11:00-13:00 IST ([script](../scripts/log_option_quotes.py)); widen it to the R2 wing strikes and the monthly expiry if it does not cover them, run it long enough to fill delta, DTE and VIX-tercile buckets, then re-cost baseline and F1 at the measured multiplier. If the measured multiplier exceeds the break-even of every candidate structure, stop signal work and treat the weekly track as unsupported by construction. Step 2 is R2 and R3, run once, as a pre-registered grid. Step 3 is R4 to R6, the cheap incremental tests. Step 4 is the no-trade regressions for R7 to R10, closing each as soon as it fails. Step 5 is the single holdout evaluation of whatever survives. R11 and R12 stay parked until some structure has positive net expectancy.

**Pre-registration protocol.** Use the repo's existing split: development window 2019-02-11 to 2023-12-31, and a 2024-01-01 holdout through the archive end (2026-08-28) that stays untouched until the candidate list is frozen. The holdout contains the 2025-09-01 Thursday-to-Tuesday expiry cutover and only about a year of Tuesday expiries, so report the eras separately and never tune on them. Handle expiries from the calendar module and treat the 19 holiday-shifted expiry dates as expiry dates, not weekdays; the engine prices expiry-day marks at intrinsic because the archive settle on that day is the index settlement value ([CLAUDE.md](../CLAUDE.md)). Commit a written spec for each rule (definition, single parameterization, metric, pass rule) before running it, and log every run in the changelog. Keep the existing pass rule: beat the baseline at 1.5x slippage, exceed zero, have a year-cluster confidence-interval lower bound above zero, and show at least four of five positive development years. Add the following. Report all three slippage multipliers (1.0x, 1.5x, 2.0x) and the break-even multiplier. Add a week-block bootstrap, because five year clusters are weak (the changelog already says so). Add a hard cost gate from R1: a rule fails if the measured multiplier exceeds its break-even. Add a rule that an overlay improving drawdown while leaving expectancy at or below zero is rejected.

**Multiple-testing arithmetic.** If twelve rules are each tested at the 5% level, the chance that at least one passes by luck is about 46% (1 - 0.95^12, the author's calculation, assuming independence), before counting slippage multiples, parameter grids or sub-arms. The earlier exploratory pattern analysis in this repo applied a Bonferroni threshold of 0.00096 across 52 tests ([Nifty market data pattern analysis](Nifty%20market%20data%20pattern%20analysis.md)); this program is smaller but needs the same discipline. Limit the primary family to at most eight confirmatory hypotheses, each with one parameterization (R2 arms count individually), control the family with Holm adjustment, and put every grid, bucket cut or second parameter value in an exploratory appendix that cannot be promoted to the holdout. R3 and the descriptive halves of R6 to R10 are exploratory and do not spend holdout. A rule that passes development and fails the holdout is retired permanently, with no retuning. Test every chain feature for incremental value over India VIX, ATM IV and DTE, since the FlashAlpha result shows OI-derived measures can collapse into ATM IV. Add placebo controls for the OI tests, the repo's permutation tests for the entry model, and a unit assertion on cost per lot in rupees, because Vilkov's retraction came from a cost-scale error. With roughly 200 weekly trades in the development window ([changelog](../BACKTEST_CHANGELOG.md)), only large effects are detectable, and no source suggests one exists.

## Conclusion

The literature changes the program's priorities more than it supplies new signals. Evidence is richest where the repo has no data (signed flow, bid/ask, intraday dealer positions) and thinnest for the chain features the repo can compute, and the best-supported timing idea, implied minus expected realized volatility, is already in the feature set. The finding with the clearest implication is the cost result: across Santa-Clara and Saretto, Hong-Sung-Yang, Vilkov's corrected package and the Indian preprints, frictions rather than information are what separate gross premium from net losses. For Nifty, the repo's break-even slippage of 0.44x for the baseline and 1.42x for hold-to-expiry turns a literature question into a measurement question, and the quote logger is the only tool in the project that can answer it.

The second implication is about the standard of evidence for the wing and structure decisions. Nobody has published a real-spread comparison of spreads, condors and wing widths against naked short strikes, so the repo's pre-registered comparison would be new evidence in its own right and should be built to be publishable: frozen specs, a held-out period, cost multiples and break-even reporting. If the measured spreads put every structure beyond its break-even, the correct result is a negative one, and the literature supports reaching it without adding signals. Two caveats remain. The notes are secondary throughout, so the eleven priority papers should be read before any figure here is cited. And the sources do not settle whether Nifty's short-premium results are cost-driven or tail-driven, which is why R3 and R1 come before every signal test.
