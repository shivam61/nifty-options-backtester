## 1. Individual-stock smirk and risk-neutral skewness: what do they predict, at what horizon, and how robust?

### Takeaway
In US single-stock data from roughly 1996-2012, steep put-side smirks and expensive puts relative to calls predict lower subsequent stock returns (Xing et al. 10.9% per year; Cremers-Weinbaum 50 bp per week; Yan 1.9% per month). The sign for risk-neutral skewness itself is contested: Conrad et al. find negative-skew stocks earn more, while Stilger et al., Chordia et al. and Rehman-Vilkov find the opposite sign. Replication evidence is thin and points to decay.

### Cited Findings
- Xing, Zhang and Zhao (JFQA 45(3), 2010): stocks with the steepest smirks underperform those with the flattest by about 10.9% per year on a risk-adjusted basis, and predictability lasts at least six months. — [CXO summary of the paper](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/?p=1798); [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/cup/jfinqa/v45y2010i03p641-662_00.html)
- The authors' mechanism: informed traders with negative news buy OTM puts, and the equity market is slow to incorporate the information in smirks. Steep-smirk firms get the worst earnings shocks in the following quarter. — [CXO summary](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/?p=1798)
- The smirk measure in a 2008 working-paper summary is the IV of OTM puts minus the IV of ATM calls, sample 1996-2005 (all firms with listed options). The working-paper summary was a secondary source. — [CXO/search summary of working paper](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/?p=1798)
- Quantpedia lists the Xing et al. idea as strategy #123 ("Options Skewness Predicts Consecutive Stocks Returns"): weekly rebalancing, equities, 1996-2005 backtest, indicative performance 9.19%, estimated volatility 11.40%. — [Quantpedia 22 Nov 2011](https://vvv.quantpedia.com/?p=481)
- Cremers and Weinbaum (JFQA 45(2), 2010): stocks with relatively expensive calls outperform stocks with relatively expensive puts by 50 bp per week. Abnormal performance appears on both sides and is not explained by short-sale constraints. Predictability is larger when option liquidity is high and stock liquidity is low, and "decreases over the sample period", which the authors read as mispricing that gradually diminished. — [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/cup/jfinqa/v45y2010i02p335-367_00.html). A working-paper version quoted 51 bp per week. — [Quantpedia](https://vvv.quantpedia.com/?p=490)
- Yan (JFE 2011): across more than 4,000 stocks 1996-2005, the lowest-smile-slope quintile beat the highest by 1.9% per month risk-adjusted; idiosyncratic slope carries most of the predictive power. — [aggregator abstract](https://academicnewsletter.sufe.edu.cn/info/356921)
- Conrad, Dittmar and Ghysels (J. Finance 2013): using BKM model-free moments, "more ex ante negatively (positively) skewed returns yield subsequent higher (lower) returns", with volatility negatively and kurtosis positively related to returns. — [search summary of abstract](https://pages.stern.nyu.edu/~dbackus/Disasters/ConradDittmarGhysels%20skewness%20Dec%2009.PDF)
- Opposite sign: Stilger, Kostakis and Poon (Management Science 2017), 1996-2012, long-high-RNS minus short-low-RNS quintile earns a Fama-French-Carhart alpha of 55 bp per month (t = 2.47), concentrated in overpriced, costly-to-short names. — [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/inm/ormnsc/v63y2017i6p1814-1834.html)
- Opposite sign: Chordia, Lin and Xiang (JFQA 2021), high-minus-low RNS differential of 0.17% per week, attributed to informed trading, and they position it against Conrad et al. — [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/cup/jfinqa/v56y2021i5p1713-1737_7.html). Rehman-Vilkov (2012 WP) also reported a positive relation, and Kim-Park found a negative relation only in certain periods (both via citing papers). — [search summary](https://repository.hku.hk/bitstream/10722/284759/1/content.pdf)
- Bali and Hovakimian (Management Science 2009): volatility levels do not predict returns, but the realized-implied spread is negatively related and the call-put IV spread positively related to returns. — [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/inm/ormnsc/v55y2009i11p1797-1812.html)
- Bali and Murray (JFQA 2013): delta- and vega-neutral "skewness assets" show a strong negative relation between RNS and returns, with no support for a risk-based explanation. — [OptionMetrics](https://optionmetrics.com/research/t-g-bali-and-s-murrary-does-risk-neutral-skewness-predict-the-cross-section-of-equity-option-portfolio-returns)
- Decay/replication: an unreviewed arXiv preprint (Li and Wang, 2026) re-estimates the Xing et al. smirk on 2015-2026 US data: the one-month univariate coefficient is significant in 2015-2019, insignificant in 2023-2026, and turns positive at three months. — [arXiv 2608.26115 listing](https://ideas.repec.org/p/arx/papers/2608.26115.html). A search-tool summary of the same preprint said the replication shows the same sign at about half the magnitude; that "half" figure did not appear in the abstract summary I retrieved and is unverified.
- Indian single-stock event evidence: for 42 negative credit-rating actions on Nifty 500 stocks, the 25-delta put-call IV skew before the event negatively predicts post-announcement CARs (beta -0.315, t = -3.84, adj. R2 0.389). Small-sample event study, not a return forecast. — [Business Perspectives](https://businessperspectives.org/journals/investment-management-and-financial-innovations/issue-518/equity-option-implied-volatility-skew-as-a-substitute-credit-risk-signal-evidence-from-negative-rating-announcements-in-india-2024-2025)

### Inferences
- The "put-side expensive then stock falls" result (Xing, Cremers-Weinbaum, Yan, Bali-Murray) is consistent across several authors on the same US 1996-2005 data, so it is not independent replication; the same OptionMetrics sample underlies most of them.
- The conflict over the sign of RNS (Conrad vs Stilger/Chordia) suggests the answer depends on the measure used (smirk or put-call spread vs full BKM RNS), the horizon (weekly vs monthly) and the sample. The put-call parity/smirk family agrees with itself; BKM RNS does not.
- All of these are cross-sectional stock-selection results holding stock-level information; they do not by themselves say anything about Nifty's own side-selection.

### Gaps
- No full text read, so exact smirk definitions (delta bands, maturity), Fama-MacBeth t-stats, weekly vs monthly rebalancing and transaction-cost treatment for Xing et al., Conrad et al. and Cremers-Weinbaum are all missing.
- Conrad et al. decile spreads, t-statistics and sample period not retrieved.
- Whether any of these cross-sectional strategies survive option bid-ask costs: not found in any retrieved source.
- Out-of-sample replication on non-US stock options: not found.

## 2. Index-level evidence: do index skew, risk-neutral skewness or the CBOE SKEW index predict index returns, realised volatility or crashes?

### Takeaway
Index-level skew is mostly a measure of priced hedging demand and sentiment, not a reliable forecaster. The best-supported index result is a short-horizon (daily to about one month) negative relation between the OTM-put-minus-ATM-call IV spread and next-period S&P 500 return (Atilgan-Bali-Demirtas), while SKEW-index return prediction is weak and unstable in everything I could retrieve.

### Cited Findings
- Atilgan, Bali and Demirtas (JBES 2015): negative relation between the IV spread and expected aggregate market returns at daily and weekly horizons; stronger around earnings announcements and extreme sentiment; robust to controls for conditional volatility, the variance risk premium and macro variables; a strategy based on it earned more than a passive S&P 500 position "after transaction costs" (abstract wording). — [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/taf/jnlbes/v33y2015i1p87-101.html)
- Secondary summary of the same paper: spread defined as S&P 500 OTM put IV minus ATM call IV; significance extends to about one month and vanishes by three months; a 1% rise in the daily spread goes with a 0.02% to 0.10% drop in next-week returns. Unverified against the paper. — [CXO Advisory](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/volatility-effects/predicting-stock-market-returns-with-implied-index-volatilities)
- Doran, Peterson and Tarrant (J. Futures Markets 2007), all S&P 100 options 1984-2006: skew shape signals crashes or upward spikes with significant probability, the spike prediction is not economically significant, the signal is strongest for short-term OTM puts, and power decreases with time to maturity. They conclude there is a large short-term jump premium explaining the negative skew of short-maturity options. — [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/wly/jfutmk/v27y2007i10p921-959.html)
- Han (RFS 2008): S&P 500 smile slope steepens and monthly RNS becomes more negative under bearish sentiment, and the relation strengthens with limits to arbitrage; so index skew partly reflects sentiment/demand pressure, not only crash expectations. — [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/oup/rfinst/v21y2008i1p387-414.html)
- Bakshi, Kapadia and Madan (RFS 2003): on OEX options and 30 stocks, individual risk-neutral distributions are far less negatively skewed than the index's, so the index skew is structurally steeper than single-stock skew. — [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/oup/rfinst/v16y2003i1p101-143.html)
- Kozhan, Neuberger and Schneider (RFS 2013): the skew risk premium accounts for over 40% of the slope of the S&P 500 implied-volatility curve; strategies capturing skew while hedging variance exposure, or the reverse, earn an insignificant premium. — [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/oup/rfinst/v26y2013i9p2174-2203.html)
- CBOE SKEW: a 30-day measure of tail risk in S&P 500 log-returns; 100 corresponds to a normal distribution and higher values put more weight on the left tail. — [search summary of Cboe description](https://www.supermoney.com/encyclopedia/skew-finance)
- SKEW predictive power, null/weak (blog and student-thesis level only): one analysis found no clear link between SKEW quartiles and later SPX or VIX moves at 1 week, 2 weeks and 1 month. — [R-bloggers "SKEWed perceptions"](https://www.r-bloggers.com/2019/12/skewed-perceptions/). A cited check by Charlie Bilello found that none of the worst one-day S&P 500 declines since 1990 had a SKEW reading in the top 5% of history in the prior month. — [SuperMoney](https://www.supermoney.com/encyclopedia/skew-finance). A practitioner page says the SKEW-return relationship changes sign by decade. — [thetrading.tools](https://www.thetrading.tools/skew-index)
- SKEW predictive power, positive: a National Chengchi University thesis reports SKEW predicts S&P 500 ETF returns especially weekly, but finds the SKEW-index correlation unstable and recommends pairing it with VIX. Single unreviewed thesis. — [NCCU](https://thesis.lib.nccu.edu.tw/detail/2ac7f9b06421fb0d0ced835df472bb71/)
- A paper on commodity IV smirks reports they predict monthly commodity and S&P 500 returns (crude oil risk-neutral fourth cumulant is the strongest S&P 500 predictor). — [Otago listing](https://ourarchive.otago.ac.nz/esploro/outputs/journalArticle/The-implied-volatility-smirk-of-commodity/9926494696301891). Not index-skew evidence.
- Indian index: I found no study testing whether Nifty index risk-neutral skewness forecasts Nifty returns. The only index-shape item was an older study reporting a classical U-shaped smile for NSE Nifty options. — [Emerald JIBR](https://www.doi.org/10.1108/JIBR-12-2013-0103)

### Inferences
- The Atilgan-Bali-Demirtas result and the Doran et al. crash result agree on direction and on short horizons, and both fade with maturity; both are in US data. Neither is evidence about India.
- "Not economically significant" (Doran et al.) and the SKEW-index nulls both point to skew levels being a poor standalone timing signal for index crashes.
- Han (2008) implies that index skew steepness is partly sentiment/demand, so an extreme skew is likelier to mean "puts rich because of hedging demand" than "crash coming".

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed full-sample test of SKEW index returns predictability was retrieved; Cboe's SKEW white paper and any Bondarenko work on SKEW were not found in search results.
- Time-series S&P 500 evidence on risk reversals or 25-delta skew predicting realized volatility: not found.
- Effect sizes and post-cost net returns for index-level timing rules: only the abstract-level statement from Atilgan et al.
- Any Nifty or other emerging-market index skew prediction study: not found.

## 3. Which strikes or deltas carry the richest premium, and is selling rich puts / buying cheap wings supported?

### Takeaway
Evidence supports that index puts are expensive on average and that skew itself is a priced premium (Kozhan et al.), but there is a real conflict on whether OTM puts are mispriced (Bondarenko, Bollen-Whaley) or statistically consistent with standard models given tail risk (Broadie-Chernov-Johannes). I could not retrieve a clean comparison of 25-delta vs ATM short-put risk-adjusted returns.

### Cited Findings
- Bondarenko ("Why are put options so expensive?", QJF 2014): no model within the studied class can explain the put anomaly. One summary gives monthly returns of roughly 39% for ATM puts and 95% for deep OTM puts, plus Jensen's alpha of 23% per month for selling ATM puts. — [search summary / IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/wsi/qjfxxx/v04y2014i03ns2010139214500153.html). **Conflict in secondary sources:** a different CXO-based summary states the numbers the other way round (39% for deep OTM and 95% for ATM). — [CXO Advisory moneyness summary](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/equity-options/moneyness-and-the-profitability-of-shorting-equity-options). Which is right is unresolved; do not cite either until checked against the paper.
- Broadie, Chernov and Johannes (RFS 2009): the large returns from writing OTM puts are statistically insignificant relative to Black-Scholes or Heston once sampling uncertainty is accounted for, while ATM straddle returns remain harder to explain; long positions generate large negative returns (for example about -30% per month for ATM puts). — [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=965739); [NBER WP PDF listing](https://m.nber.org/conferences/2007/si2007/AP/chernov.pdf)
- Bollen and Whaley (2004), quoted in Broadie et al.: significantly positive abnormal returns for selling options across strikes, with the lowest strikes (OTM puts) most profitable. — [Columbia copy of BCJ](https://drupalgsb-test.paas.cc.columbia.edu/sites/default/files-efs/pubfiles/3964/broadie_chernov_johannes.pdf)
- Kozhan, Neuberger and Schneider: the skew premium is more than 40% of the S&P 500 implied-vol-curve slope, but capturing skew with variance hedged (or vice versa) earns an insignificant premium. So the "extra" premium of OTM puts over ATM is not separately significant once variance risk is hedged. — [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/oup/rfinst/v26y2013i9p2174-2203.html)
- Tosi and Ziegler (2017), via CXO: shorting S&P 500 OTM puts one week before expiry and holding to expiry gives an average annual return of 8.7% with annual Sharpe ratio 2.8, with rare steep drawdowns; results generally hold when delta-hedged with futures. — [CXO Advisory](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/short-selling/when-to-sell-equity-index-put-options/)
- Bondarenko's later volatility-risk-premium framing: from 1990 to 2018 average VIX was 19.3% vs average realized volatility of 15.1%. — [Cboe insights](https://www.cboe.com/insights/posts/white-paper-shows-volatility-risk-premium-facilitated-higher-risk-adjusted-returns-for-put-index)
- Russell 2000 one-month covered calls: Sharpe 0.27 (ATM), 0.33 (2% OTM), 0.37 (5% OTM) vs 0.23 for the index; call-side only. — [CXO Advisory](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/equity-options/russell-2000-index-buy-write-strategy-performance)
- Bakshi-Kapadia-Madan: index risk-neutral skew is much more negative than for individual stocks. — [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/oup/rfinst/v16y2003i1p101-143.html)

### Inferences
- "Sell the rich side" is supported in terms of average premium, but whether the OTM put premium is a free lunch or fair tail compensation is unresolved: BCJ say the realized sample is too short to tell; Bondarenko says it cannot be explained.
- "Buy cheap wings" is not directly tested in anything I retrieved. If the OTM put wing is where the skew premium sits, buying it to define risk gives away the richest premium; this is a hypothesis, not a finding.
- The call side of the Nifty structure is the cheap side by the same logic (index skew is negative), so the literature suggests the premium collected per unit of delta is lower on calls.

### Gaps
- A clean source comparing 25-delta vs ATM vs deeper OTM index short-put returns after bid-ask costs: not found.
- Evidence on iron condors, put spreads or butterfly (defined-risk) structures by delta: not found.
- India/Nifty evidence on which strikes carry the richest premium vs realized tail: not found. The user's own chain-engine result (negative expectancy at real EOD settle prices, per CLAUDE.md) is the only Nifty evidence available in this project.

## 4. Replication, decay, sample dependence and conflicts

### Takeaway
The cross-sectional smirk/put-call-spread effects look strongest in 1996-2005 data and weaken afterwards by the authors' own account (Cremers-Weinbaum) and in a recent unreviewed replication; the sign of risk-neutral skewness itself is contested across the top journals.

### Cited Findings
- Cremers-Weinbaum: predictability decreases over the sample, consistent with mispricing that gradually diminished. — [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/cup/jfinqa/v45y2010i02p335-367_00.html)
- Li and Wang preprint: smirk coefficient significant 2015-2019, insignificant 2023-2026, positive at three months (unreviewed). — [IDEAS listing](https://ideas.repec.org/p/arx/papers/2608.26115.html)
- RNS sign conflict: Conrad et al. (negative) vs Stilger et al., Chordia et al., Rehman-Vilkov (positive), Kim-Park (negative only in certain periods). — [search summaries](https://repository.hku.hk/bitstream/10722/284759/1/content.pdf)
- The sample years of most of the strongest results are 1996-2005 (Xing, Yan) or 1996-2012 (Stilger); that overlap is a sample-dependence flag.
- Conflict on Bondarenko's moneyness numbers across secondary summaries (see Section 3).
- SKEW-index evidence: a thesis finds weekly predictability; blogs and a practitioner page find null or sign-changing results. — [NCCU](https://thesis.lib.nccu.edu.tw/detail/2ac7f9b06421fb0d0ced835df472bb71/), [thetrading.tools](https://www.thetrading.tools/skew-index)

### Inferences
- Treat skew-based stock-level alpha as a decayed, sample-dependent anomaly; do not extrapolate its magnitude to an index like Nifty.
- Where papers disagree in sign, the disagreement is itself informative: skew has both an information component (informed put buying) and a premium component (hedging demand), and they act in opposite directions on returns.

### Gaps
- No independent peer-reviewed replication outside the US was retrieved.
- Magnitude of decay after 2005 cannot be quantified from the abstracts.

## 5. Implications for chain-based strike/side selection in a Nifty backtester

### Takeaway
The literature gives low-to-moderate support for using skew as a side-tilt or timing filter on an index, and almost none for a quantified "delta shift" rule; everything below is a testable hypothesis, not an established edge. All proposed features need only EOD settle and OI per strike (no bid/ask), but they inherit the EOD-settle noise and the lack of intraday data.

### Cited Findings
- Index skew is structurally negative and steeper than single-stock skew. — [Bakshi-Kapadia-Madan](https://ideas.repec.org/a/oup/rfinst/v16y2003i1p101-143.html)
- Skew steepness varies with sentiment/hedging demand. — [Han 2008](https://ideas.repec.org/a/oup/rfinst/v21y2008i1p387-414.html)
- A higher OTM-put-minus-ATM-call IV spread on the S&P 500 goes with lower returns over days to about a month. — [Atilgan-Bali-Demirtas](https://ideas.repec.org/a/taf/jnlbes/v33y2015i1p87-101.html)
- Short-maturity OTM-put skew shape is the part that signals crashes, and the signal fades with maturity. — [Doran et al.](https://ideas.repec.org/a/wly/jfutmk/v27y2007i10p921-959.html)
- Over 40% of the S&P 500 IV slope is a priced skew premium. — [Kozhan et al.](https://ideas.repec.org/a/oup/rfinst/v26y2013i9p2174-2203.html)
- Index-put-selling returns are not statistically distinguishable from model-consistent returns in a short sample. — [BCJ](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=965739)

### Inferences (testable features and rules; evidence strength is my assessment)
1. **Put-minus-call skew spread as a side-tilt feature.** Compute from EOD settle-implied IVs on the Nifty chain: IV(OTM put near 25-delta) minus IV(ATM) and IV(OTM call near 25-delta) minus IV(ATM), or a smirk = IV(OTM put) - IV(ATM call) in the Xing/Atilgan style. Hypothesis: when the spread is high, forward index returns are lower over days to a month, so favour keeping calls further out or skipping the call-sell side and shorten the put-side exposure. Evidence strength: moderate for S&P 500 at daily to weekly horizon (one abstract plus a secondary summary), weak for transferability to Nifty (no Indian index evidence found), and weak for sign stability post-2005 (decay noted for stock-level). Data: EOD settle only. Caveat: backing IV out of EOD settle (and expiry-day intrinsic) is noisy; low-OI strikes should be filtered by the existing archive rule (0 contracts and 0 OI skipped).
2. **Skew z-score regime filter rather than a level.** Rank the current put-skew against its own trailing window (for example one year) instead of using an absolute threshold, because index skew steepens with sentiment (Han) and SKEW-index levels are unstable across decades. Evidence strength: low (inference from Han, plus SKEW-index sign-instability anecdotes). Data: EOD only.
3. **Premium-per-delta by strike (richest-side check).** Compute realized P&L per lot for short puts vs short calls at fixed deltas (for example 10/15/25) using the existing chain engine, and stratify by skew tercile. This tests whether the rich side really pays after the realized tail on Nifty. Evidence strength: the literature is conflicted on whether OTM put richness is mispricing (Bondarenko) or compensation (BCJ, Kozhan et al.), so this is an empirical question for the user's own data rather than something to assume. Data: EOD only.
4. **Delta shift rule.** A rule of the form "when the put skew is in the top tercile, move the short-put strike further OTM by one delta step (or reduce put-side width)" has no direct quantified support in anything retrieved. Doran et al. imply the short-dated OTM put signal is the informative part, and that the signal weakens with longer maturity. Evidence strength: very low; treat as exploratory.
5. **Defined-risk wings.** Whether to buy the cheap or the rich wing is not tested in the retrieved sources. Kozhan et al.'s insignificant premium once variance is hedged suggests the extra skew carry may largely be variance premium; the right test is wing-width ablation in the chain engine. Evidence strength: none direct.
6. **Do not use the CBOE-style SKEW formula as a timing signal on Nifty without validating it.** The index-level SKEW evidence is mixed and mostly non-peer-reviewed. A Nifty SKEW-like number can be built from EOD settle strikes but needs enough liquid OTM strikes; the archive's EOD-only, no bid/ask nature limits the quality of the tails.
7. **Costs.** None of the retrieved sources gave a cost-adjusted return for a skew rule except the abstract-level claim in Atilgan et al. Costs for Nifty (no bid/ask in the archive) would have to be assumed via the existing slippage multipliers (1.0x/1.5x/2.0x per the project CLAUDE.md). The project's own chain-engine result of negative expectancy at all three slippage multipliers sets a high bar: a skew tilt would need to shift expectancy by more than roughly 177 to 348 Rs/lot just to reach zero, which the retrieved literature gives no basis to expect.

### Gaps
- Whether intraday data would help: the retrieved literature uses daily or weekly data, so I found no evidence that intraday skew adds anything. The only intraday-sensitive point is that short-maturity put skew is the informative part (Doran et al.), and EOD settle is a coarse proxy for it.
- No source quantified a "delta shift" or a skew-conditioned strike choice for index short-premium structures.
- Nifty-specific skew predictability and the effect of the Thursday-to-Tuesday expiry cutover on skew features were not researched here.
- All numeric claims above rest on abstracts and secondary summaries; the original papers should be opened (all fetch routes were blocked in this session) before any figure is used in a report.
