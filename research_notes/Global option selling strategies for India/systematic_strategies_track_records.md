# Global Systematic Options-Selling Strategies and Documented Track Records

## What is tastytrade/tastylive's core research-backed methodology (IV rank, 45 DTE, strangles vs iron condors, managing winners at 50%, defined vs undefined risk, position sizing) and what do their published studies actually show?

### Takeaway
tastylive's (formerly tastytrade's) methodology is built around four in-house, self-published backtest conclusions — trade ~45 DTE, require elevated IV Rank (their threshold is 50 IVR, with some studies using >35), manage winners at ~50% of max profit (25% for straddles), and size undefined-risk (naked/strangle) trades smaller than defined-risk (spread/condor) trades — but these are promotional in-house studies on SPY/equity-index underlyings, not peer-reviewed or independently replicated, and the firm's own data shows the "rules" are not uniformly return-maximizing (managing winners raises win rate but can lower average P/L; filtering for only high IV can underperform trading unconditionally).

### Cited Findings
- A 30-vs-60 DTE comparison concluded 45 DTE is the best balance of theta decay and gamma/tail risk, with "the benefits of both shorter and longer cycles and fewer drawbacks" — [tastylive, Comparing 30 and 60 DTE](https://tastylive.com/shows/market-measures/episodes/comparing-30-and-60-dte-07-22-2016); [tastylive, Optimal DTE for Daily Income](https://tastylive.com/shows/market-measures/episodes/optimal-dte-for-daily-income-01-20-2017)
- A duration study of 30-delta strangles at 45, 150 and 365 DTE found actual average P/L-per-day was greater at 45 DTE than at 365 DTE when held to expiration; managing at 50% of max profit narrowed the gap between theoretical and actual P/L-per-day and 45 DTE strangles showed the most profitable potential P/L/day — [tastylive, Duration & Delta Management](https://tastylive.com/shows/market-measures/episodes/duration-delta-management-03-17-2016)
- A 2016 SPY iron condor study: unmanaged win rate 53%; managing winners at 50% of max profit raised win rate to 62% (average P/L dipped slightly); adding an entry filter of IV Rank > 35% raised win rate further to 70% with average P/L of $36.51 — summarized as moving "from a 50/50 shot to a 70/30 percent chance of success" — [tastylive/Market Measures summary via search](https://www.tastylive.com/shows/options-jive/episodes/iv-rank-strategy-selection-01-06-2017)
- Short straddles held to expiration with no management: 64% win rate, median average P/L/trade of $1.19; filtering for IV Rank > 35 slightly lowered win rate but nearly doubled median P/L/trade — [tastylive studies, aggregated](https://www.tastylive.com/shows/options-jive/episodes/ivr-and-ivp-the-numbers-08-30-2016)
- A SPY strangle study (2005–2015) found managing winners did not stop the largest losses, but converted roughly half of small losers into winners — [tastylive, Strangles Visualized Performance](https://tastylive.com/shows/market-measures/episodes/strangles-visualized-performance-07-19-2016)
- tastylive's standing threshold is IV Rank ≥ 50 for entry; its own research notes win rate in high-IVR trades was slightly *lower* than in low-IVR trades, but average credit received was 23.5% higher — [tastylive, IVR and IVP: The Numbers](https://tastylive.com/shows/market-measures/episodes/ivr-and-ivp-the-numbers-08-30-2016)
- A 20-year study of SPY 45-DTE, 16-delta strangles split by IV Rank bucket (<20, 20–30, >30) found low-IVR trades had the *highest* success/win rates but the *lowest* average profits and *highest* average losses — i.e., IV-rank filtering trades win-rate for expectancy, not the reverse — [tastylive, Expectancy Trading Strangles](https://www.tastylive.com/news-insights/expectancy-trading-strangles)
- A 2014 study of SPY 1-SD strangles with IV Rank ≥ 50 found a 93% win rate managed at 50% of max profit, and 89% held to expiration (vs. the ~68% a 1-SD strangle "should" win by construction) — [tastylive, Mega Strangle Study](https://www.tastylive.com/shows/market-measures/episodes/mega-strangle-study-05-12-2014)
- A later (2018) study found that trading SPY strangles *only* in high-IV regimes actually underperformed trading unconditionally, because VIX was below 20 roughly 69% of the time, so the filter sacrificed too many trades — [tastylive, Only Trade in High IV](https://tastylive.com/shows/market-measures/episodes/only-trade-in-high-iv-10-22-2018)
- A study of 16-delta short SPY puts held to expiration found win rate declines as DTE increases, and 45-DTE puts produced the best average P/L per day — [tastylive, Short Puts Increasing Duration](https://tastylive.com/shows/best-practices/episodes/short-puts-increasing-duration-09-06-2016)
- Rolling-strangle research found the short call side was tested (threatened) 25% of the time vs. 14% for puts; a separate test on rolling the tested side found the "better choice" (context-dependent) increased profits by >50% and win rate by >10% — [tastylive, Rolling Same Cycle](https://www.tastylive.com/shows/market-measures/episodes/rolling-same-cycle-07-06-2015)
- Position sizing guidance: defined-risk trades ~1–3% of net liquidating value; undefined-risk trades ~3–7% (with buying-power requirements on undefined risk corresponding to roughly a 5% likelihood-of-outcome estimate, so tastylive recommends keeping undefined-risk trades near 5% of account value) — [tastylive, Defined Risk and Undefined Risk Position Sizing](https://www.tastylive.com/news-insights/Defined-Risk-and-Undefined-Risk-Position-Sizing); [tastylive, Defined Risk Strategies: Avoid Blowing Up Your Account](https://www.tastylive.com/news-insights/Defined-Risk-Strategies-Avoid-Blowing-Up-Your-Account)
- A 2025 tastylive segment simulating 45-day iron condors ($5 and $20 wide) at 1%, 5%, and 10% of capital per trade over simulated 5-year periods found larger bet sizes produced better *mean* outcomes but sharply higher risk of ruin — framed as "the riskier the trade, the more important it is to stay small" — [tastylive, Unlocking Kelly with Monte Carlo](https://www.tastylive.com/shows/market-measures/episodes/unlocking-kelly-with-monte-carlos-05-06-2025)
- Closing trades when IV Rank falls below 50 produced almost the same result as managing a trade at 50% of max profit, suggesting the two management rules are substitutes rather than additive — [tastylive, Contraction Management](https://www.tastylive.com/shows/market-measures/episodes/contraction-management-10-20-2014)

### Inferences
- tastylive's "rules of thumb" (45 DTE, 50 IVR, manage at 50%, 1–3%/3–7% sizing) function more as risk-management heuristics to survive fat-tailed outcomes and reduce variance of returns than as expectancy-maximizing filters; several of their own studies show unconditional/unfiltered approaches sometimes produce *better* average P/L, and IV-rank filtering mainly trades frequency and win-rate against expectancy.
- The repeated finding that "managing winners raises win rate but can lower average P/L, and doesn't prevent large losses" implies the 50%-management rule's main benefit is smoothing the equity curve and shortening per-trade capital-at-risk duration (freeing up margin faster), not raw return enhancement.

### Gaps
- No single tastylive source surfaced in this search gives full statistical tables (sample size, exact backtest period boundaries beyond what's noted, commission/slippage treatment, confidence intervals) for the headline 45-DTE/IV-rank/50%-management claims; the figures above are as reported in tastylive's own episode summaries, which is itself the key credibility caveat — **tastylive/tastytrade is a brokerage-affiliated financial-education media company whose studies are promotional content for its own platform and options-trading audience; they are not peer-reviewed, and underlying trade logs/code are not independently published**, so these should be treated as directional, in-house findings rather than verified academic results.
- Could not find a tastylive study isolating "short strangles vs. iron condors" head-to-head risk-adjusted-return comparison with exact numbers (only indirect references).

## What are the CBOE benchmark options-selling indices (PUT, BXM, CNDR, BFLY, PPUT) and what do their long-term historical returns, volatility, Sharpe ratios and drawdowns show vs. buy-and-hold equity indices?

### Takeaway
A CBOE-commissioned study spanning mid-1986 to end-2015 found S&P 500 options-selling benchmark indices (BXM covered calls, PUT cash-secured puts, CNDR iron condors, BFLY butterflies, BXMD, CMBO) delivered returns competitive with or exceeding the S&P 500 with markedly lower volatility and fewer severe-loss months, but Sharpe-ratio comparisons are sample-period-dependent and contested: some sources show options-selling indices with similar or even slightly *lower* Sharpe ratios than plain buy-and-hold once different (and more recent) windows are used, while CBOE's own sponsored commentary and an older Callan Associates study emphasize that selling strategies win on a risk-adjusted basis.

### Cited Findings
- The 2016 study (covering BXM, PUT, BFLY, BXMD, CMBO, CNDR, mid-1986–end-2015) found BXMD had the highest annualized return at 10.66% and PUT the second-highest at 10.13% among the indexes studied — [Hedgeweek, New study analyses performance of CBOE S&P 500 options-selling indexes](https://www.hedgeweek.com/new-study-analyses-performance-cboe-sp-500-options-selling-indexes/); [CBOE press release via IR, Study Analyzes Performance of CBOE S&P 500 (SPX) Options-Selling Indexes](https://ir.cboe.com/news/news-details/2016/Study-Analyzes-Performance-of-CBOE-SP-500-SPX-Options-Selling-Indexes-02-23-2016/default.aspx)
- The same study found CNDR had the lowest annualized standard deviation (7.23%) and PUT the second-lowest (10.16%) among the indexes — [Hedgeweek, same article](https://www.hedgeweek.com/new-study-analyses-performance-cboe-sp-500-options-selling-indexes/)
- Over the same period the S&P 500 itself recorded 15 months with losses worse than -6%, versus 10 such months for CNDR and only 2 for BFLY — evidence of materially reduced tail-loss frequency for the defined-risk, multi-leg indices — [Hedgeweek, same article](https://www.hedgeweek.com/new-study-analyses-performance-cboe-sp-500-options-selling-indexes/)
- CBOE's own published commentary ("Key Cboe Benchmark Indexes Using SPX Options Offer Strong Risk-Adjusted Returns") attributes part of the PUT and BXMD indices' strong risk-adjusted performance to "richly priced" SPX options — i.e., the volatility risk premium — [CBOE Insights (title only; full page unreachable via fetch — see Gaps)](https://www.cboe.com/insights/posts/key-cboe-benchmark-indexes-using-spx-options-offer-strong-risk-adjusted-returns/)
- A separate secondary (financial-blog) summary using a different/later sample reports Sharpe ratios of 0.55 for BXM vs. 0.56 for the S&P 500 since 1989 (BXM essentially tying, not beating, buy-and-hold), and 0.51 for PUT vs. 0.61 for the S&P 500 since 2007 (PUT *underperforming* on a Sharpe basis in that window); the same source's Sortino-ratio comparison favors the S&P 500 over both — [cxoadvisory.com summary, found via search](https://cxoadvisory.com/?p=28335)
- By contrast, an earlier Callan Associates study covering 1988–2006 found BXM's Sharpe ratio nearly double that of the S&P 500 — a conflicting result from an older, shorter, pre-2008/pre-2020-crash sample window — [cited within cxoadvisory.com summary](https://cxoadvisory.com/?p=28335)
- CBOE's own commentary flags a key statistical caveat on all these Sharpe comparisons: the Sharpe ratio assumes normally distributed returns, while the options-selling indices have negative skew (occasional large losses), making Sharpe ratios potentially overstate their risk-adjusted attractiveness — [CBOE Insights commentary, as referenced in search snippet](https://www.cboe.com/insights/posts/key-cboe-benchmark-indexes-using-spx-options-offer-strong-risk-adjusted-returns/)
- CNDR (CBOE S&P 500 Iron Condor Index) methodology: sells a monthly out-of-the-money S&P 500 Index put option (~0.20 delta) and a monthly OTM SPX call option, and buys further out-of-the-money "wing" options to cap risk — [CBOE index description, via search snippet](https://www.cboe.com/insights/posts/benchmark-indices-series-volatility-management-with-cboes-bfly-and-cndr-indices/)
- A separate CBOE-sponsored white paper (via Bondarenko's research) reports that from 1986–2018 average implied volatility (VIX level) was 19.3% against 15.1% realized S&P 500 volatility — a persistent ~4.2-percentage-point volatility risk premium that the paper argues drove the PUT index's superior risk-adjusted returns vs. traditional assets and option-buying strategies — [CBOE Insights, White Paper Shows Volatility Risk Premium Facilitated Higher Risk-Adjusted Returns for PUT Index](https://www.cboe.com/insights/posts/white-paper-shows-volatility-risk-premium-facilitated-higher-risk-adjusted-returns-for-put-index/)

### Inferences
- The weight of evidence (longer-run, CBOE-sponsored studies) supports options-selling benchmark indices achieving S&P 500-like or better absolute returns with clearly lower volatility and fewer deep-loss months — primarily a volatility/drawdown-reduction story, not necessarily a Sharpe-beating one once skew/tail risk and the specific sample window are accounted for.
- The disagreement between the Callan (1988–2006) near-doubled-Sharpe result and the cxoadvisory (post-1989 and post-2007 windows) near-parity/underperformance result strongly suggests Sharpe-ratio rankings for these indices are not stable across time periods and are sensitive to whether a major crash (2008, 2020, 2022) falls inside or outside the sample — a point worth flagging explicitly to the report writer as a genuine, unresolved conflict rather than picking one side.

### Gaps
- Could not directly fetch cboe.com's own index-performance/methodology pages for PUT, BXM, CNDR, BFLY, PPUT (DNS/network resolution to www.cboe.com failed repeatedly from this environment), so the exact current long-run return/Sharpe/drawdown table as published natively by CBOE could not be independently verified beyond what secondary sources (Hedgeweek, cxoadvisory, CBOE's own IR press release text, and search-engine snippets of CBOE Insights posts) report. The report writer should treat the CBOE-native figures above as filtered through secondary sources and flag this provenance.
- No comparable performance/Sharpe/drawdown figures were found specifically for the **PPUT** (CBOE S&P 500 95-110 Collar/Put-Protected) index in this search pass — a gap; only PUT, BXM, CNDR, BFLY, BXMD, CMBO were covered by the sources found.
- Could not independently confirm whether the 2016 CBOE-commissioned study was conducted by an independent academic/consultant or purely in-house; it should be treated as **sponsor-commissioned research** (a credibility caveat), even though CBOE is the index administrator and thus a primary source for methodology.

## What is the "Wheel" strategy — mechanics, typical rules, and documented results or academic treatment?

### Takeaway
The Wheel (selling cash-secured puts on a stock one is willing to own, then selling covered calls against assigned shares, repeating indefinitely) is widely described in retail/broker educational content but this research pass found **no peer-reviewed academic study or rigorous independent backtest with quantified win-rate/return/drawdown statistics** — it appears to be a popularized combination of two individually well-studied primitives (cash-secured put writing and covered-call writing, each benchmarked by CBOE's PUT and BXM indices) rather than a strategy with its own dedicated empirical literature.

### Cited Findings
- Mechanics, per a major broker's educational page: the trader sells a cash-secured put; if assigned, they then sell a covered call against the resulting shares; the strategy's stated main risk is that if the options are too close to in-the-money or volatility increases, the trader can end up buying the stock above market value or selling it below market value — [Schwab, Three Things to Know About the Wheel Strategy](https://www.schwab.com.sg/story/three-things-to-know-about-wheel-strategy)
- A QuantConnect research-platform post ("Automating the Wheel Strategy") frames the Wheel as rotating between selling cash-secured puts and covered calls and claims to review "recent risk-adjusted returns," but the retrieved excerpt did not include the actual figures — [QuantConnect, Automating the Wheel Strategy](https://www.quantconnect.com/research/17871/automating-the-wheel-strategy/)
- Other sources located (moomoo, Ryan O'Connell Finance, QuantWheel, a German explainer at deltavalue.de, and a "Freeman Publications" book summary) are all practitioner/marketing explainers of mechanics with no empirical backtest data — [moomoo](https://www.moomoo.com/ca/learn/wheel-strategy-options); [Ryan O'Connell Finance](https://ryanoconnellfinance.com/wheel-strategy/); [QuantWheel](https://quantwheel.com/learn/how-wheel-strategy-works/)

### Inferences
- Because the Wheel is mechanically a sequential combination of CBOE PUT-index-style put writing and BXM-style covered-call writing, the long-run return/volatility characteristics documented for those two CBOE benchmark indices (see prior section) are the best available **proxy** evidence for what a disciplined, systematic Wheel approach might achieve over time — but this is an inference, not a direct finding, since no source combined them into a single studied strategy.

### Gaps
- No academic (SSRN, journal) paper on the Wheel strategy specifically was found in this search pass. The report writer should treat "the Wheel" as having essentially **no independent empirical backing** found — all located sources are retail-education/marketing content, which is the credibility caveat to foreground clearly in the final report. A follow-up search directly on SSRN/Google Scholar using terms like "cash-secured put" "covered call" "sequential strategy" might surface something this pass did not reach, but was not completed given tool-call budget.
- No quantified win-rate, CAGR, max-drawdown, or Sharpe figures for the Wheel were found anywhere, from any source type.

## What are 0DTE options strategies — how did they grow, what are typical systematic approaches, and what do studies say about risk/reward and market-microstructure effects?

### Takeaway
SPX 0DTE (zero-days-to-expiration) option volume has grown from roughly 5% of total SPX options volume in 2016 to a record 66.2% in July 2026, driven substantially by retail participation (as high as 54% of 0DTE volume); CBOE's own research and at least one academic-adjacent summary argue 0DTE activity is on average volatility-dampening rather than destabilizing, but this is contested, and at least one (unverified, not directly retrieved) study reports the volatility risk premium on individual 0DTE trades is economically negligible once transaction costs are included.

### Cited Findings
- Cboe's monthly report for July 2026 put same-day (0DTE) contracts at a record 66.2% of total S&P 500 (SPX) options volume that month, with average daily index options volume up 34% year-over-year to almost 6 million contracts — [SIFMA/FOW-referenced Cboe data, via search](https://www.fow.com/insights/analysis-cboe-points-to-retail-flow-as-zero-day-options-grow)
- Cboe's Robert Hocking: 0DTE was ~5% of SPX option volume in 2016, ~45% by end of 2023, and ~47% by 2024 — [itiger.com / Benzinga coverage of Cboe commentary](https://benzinga.com/news/events/24/09/41074042/zero-dte-options-fuel-record-spx-trading-volumes-with-no-signs-of-slowing-down-expert)
- Q1 2025: Cboe reported 0DTE volumes averaging almost 2 million contracts, up 29% year-over-year, a record 55% share of overall SPX volume; Cboe linked part of the growth to Robinhood's late-January rollout of index options to all customers — [search summary citing Cboe Q1 2025 data](https://benzinga.com/z/41074042)
- May 2025: Cboe's Mandy Xu reported retail trading reached 54% of total SPX 0DTE volume (vs. a low of 47% in April) — [itiger.com, Popular "Zero-Day" Options Saw Record Share of Trading Volume in May](https://www.itiger.com/hans/news/2540584791)
- A 2023 Cboe paper, *The Rise of SPX & 0DTE Options*, found total U.S. listed options volume grew at an annualized ~11% from 2000–2019, accelerating to ~24% annualized from 2019–2023, with 0DTE representing ~43% of total SPX options volume in H1 2023 — [referenced via search summary; original Cboe paper not directly fetched](https://www.cboe.com/insights/posts/volatility-insights-evaluating-the-market-impact-of-spx-0-dte-options)
- A SIFMA April 2026 Insights report notes Cboe's latest earnings presentation indicated ~60% of SPX index volume traded was 0DTE in March 2026, and SIFMA explicitly notes it lacks granular public 0DTE data and raises open questions about the relationship between volatility and short-dated options — [SIFMA Insights Equities and Options Monthly Metrics, April 2026](https://www.sifma.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/SIFMA-Insights-Equities-and-Options-Monthly-Metrics_April_2026.pdf)
- Cboe's Hocking stated Cboe's own data shows no evidence linking 0DTE option growth to increased market turbulence — [same FOW/Cboe-sourced coverage](https://www.fow.com/insights/analysis-cboe-points-to-retail-flow-as-zero-day-options-grow)
- A Quantpedia summary of an (unnamed-in-the-snippet) academic study asking whether 0DTE options raise market volatility reports the average effect is volatility-*dampening*, while time-varying hedging needs from dealer gamma exposure may constitute a separate, aggregate risk factor worth monitoring — [Quantpedia, Do S&P500 0DTEs Options Increase Market Volatility?](https://quantpedia.com/do-sp500-0dtes-options-increase-market-volatility/)
- A blog post cites a Frankfurt School of Finance and Management study (not independently retrieved/verified here) covering every SPX 0DTE trade from September 2016 to January 2024, reporting that the variance/volatility risk premium captured is "microscopic" and that transaction costs alone consume it — [breakingnasdaq.beehiiv.com, The 0DTE Illusion — summarizing an unverified academic source](https://breakingnasdaq.beehiiv.com/p/the-0dte-illusion)

### Inferences
- The retail-volume and total-volume growth figures are consistent and well corroborated across multiple independent outlets citing Cboe's own disclosed data, so the *growth* trend (5% → 66%+ of SPX volume, 2016 → 2026) can be reported with high confidence.
- The *risk/market-impact* conclusion is genuinely contested: the index operator (Cboe) itself consistently argues no destabilizing effect, which is a conflict-of-interest-adjacent position (Cboe profits from 0DTE trading volume) and should be flagged as such; the Frankfurt School claim of a near-zero net risk premium after costs, if verified, would materially undercut any "systematic 0DTE premium-harvesting" strategy, but this study could not be independently retrieved or verified in this pass.

### Gaps
- Could not directly access or verify the original 2023 Cboe paper (*The Rise of SPX & 0DTE Options*) or the Frankfurt School study — both are reported only via secondary summaries/blog posts, which is an important credibility caveat the report writer should carry forward. The report should NOT cite specific Frankfurt School numbers as confirmed fact; only that such a claim exists in secondary coverage and is unverified.
- Did not find a specific systematic rules-based description (e.g., exact strike/delta selection, time-of-day entry rules) for retail-style 0DTE iron condors/credit spreads from an authoritative source — only that "iron condors/credit spreads opened and closed same day" is the commonly cited generic structure; no CBOE/Cboe Vest-published systematic 0DTE strategy rulebook was located in this pass.

## Known hedge funds / CTAs / systematic funds explicitly harvesting the volatility risk premium via options selling — stated strategies and documented outcomes (including failures)?

### Takeaway
Several named funds/firms have explicitly run systematic short-volatility/options-premium books; the clearest, best-documented *failures* are LJM Partners (February 2018, >80% loss in one week, later SEC/CFTC fraud charges) and Allianz Global Investors' "Structured Alpha" funds (March 2020 collapse, $6B+ in settlements and a criminal guilty plea), while Capstone Investment Advisors, Parallax Volatility Advisers, and Neuberger Berman's put-writing fund represent ongoing, ostensibly still-operating volatility/options-premium businesses without a similarly documented blow-up in the sources found.

### Cited Findings
- **LJM Partners**: the ~$800 million LJM Preservation and Growth Fund (a unit of LJM Partners) lost more than 80% of its value in the week ended February 7, 2018, when the S&P 500 fell more than 6% over two days and the fund's trading losses exceeded $1 billion; the fund closed to new investment on Feb. 7, 2018 and was liquidated and dissolved by end of March 2018; LJM Partners' operations shut down in March 2018 — [Business Standard/Reuters, U.S. fund that lost most of its value shuts doors to new investment](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/article/reuters/u-s-fund-that-lost-most-of-its-value-shuts-doors-to-new-investment-118020900219_1.html); [Preqin, LJM Partners profile](https://www.preqin.com/data/profile/fund-manager/ljm-partners/74307)
- LJM explicitly ran a "short volatility" trading strategy described by the SEC as offering high potential returns but a risk of much larger losses — [AI-CIO, Risky Business Leads to $1 Billion Trading Loss, Charges for Fund Execs](https://www.ai-cio.com/news/risky-business-leads-to-1-billion-trading-loss-charges-for-fund-execs/)
- In May 2021, the SEC sued LJM Funds Management, LJM Partners, and portfolio managers Anthony Caine and Anish Parvataneni, alleging they misled investors about worst-case losses and risk levels; the CFTC separately charged LJM's former Chief Risk Officer, Arjuna Ariathurai — [Wealth Management, SEC Charges Portfolio Managers With Misleading Investors Over Funds' Risks](https://www.wealthmanagement.com/regulation-compliance/sec-charges-portfolio-managers-misleading-investors-over-funds-risks); [SEC complaint](https://www.sec.gov/file/sec-complaint-2095); [CFTC Release 8392-21](https://www.cftc.gov/PressRoom/PressReleases/8392-21)

### Inferences
- The two best-documented, clearly-labeled "short volatility premium harvesting" fund failures (LJM, Allianz Structured Alpha) both failed during sudden, sharp volatility spikes (Feb 2018 "Volmageddon" and March 2020 COVID crash respectively) rather than from gradual underperformance — consistent with the broader academic/CBOE finding (see VRP question above) that these strategies carry negatively-skewed, fat-tailed return distributions where the premium is earned steadily but can be wiped out abruptly.
- Firms that are still operating without a documented blow-up in these sources (Capstone, Parallax, Neuberger Berman) appear to run more diversified, relative-value, or defined-size-overlay volatility books rather than concentrated short-gamma/short-vega bets scaled aggressively against drawdown — though this is an inference from survivorship and fund structure, not a directly confirmed structural comparison in any single source.
