# Indian Market Context for NSE Nifty/Bank Nifty Index Options Selling

## Who are the well-known Indian systematic/quant options sellers, educators, or published frameworks around Nifty/Bank Nifty options selling, and what do they publish?

### Takeaway
The Indian retail options-selling "ecosystem" is dominated by analytics platforms (Sensibull, Quantsapp) that sell tools rather than published, falsifiable strategies, and by individual YouTube educators (most prominently P R Sundar) whose track records are unverified and who have in at least one case drawn SEBI enforcement action for unregistered advisory activity. Zerodha's Varsity is the closest thing to a neutral, widely-used educational reference, but it teaches strategy mechanics (short strangle, iron condor) generically rather than a specific proprietary Nifty/Bank Nifty system. No credible, audited long-term track record for any named Indian educator was found.

### Cited Findings
- P R Sundar is a well-known Indian options-trading YouTube educator/influencer with roughly 1.2M subscribers (as of mid-2026 per third-party tracker data), self-described as an options trader and explicitly **not SEBI-registered** — [Qoruz/speakrj channel stats](https://qoruz.com/P_R_Sundar/youtube), [storyboard18 profile](https://www.storyboard18.com/brand-makers/who-is-pr-sundar-mercedes-and-jaguar-owning-options-trader-living-in-a-rs-30-crore-penthouse-3659.htm)

- Forum discussion (TradingQnA) describes the general pattern among YouTube option-selling educators including P R Sundar and "Optionables": sellers target ~3–4% returns via active "firefighting"/adjustment of losing legs, with far-OTM option hedges rather than static defined-risk structures — this is anecdotal forum commentary, not an official or audited source — [TradingQnA thread](https://tradingqna.com/t/trading-profit-shown-in-youtube-and-various-contradictory-aspects-found/120381)

- Sensibull's own 2018 marketing blog post states that "less than 1% of options traders make any money in the long run" — a company statement, not independent research, but notable as a platform's own framing of retail outcomes — [Zerodha Z-Connect](https://zerodha.com/z-connect/tradezerodha/sensibull/introducing-sensibull-the-options-trading-platform)

- A retail Udemy course on "Nifty Option Selling Monthly and Weekly" teaches short strangles with adjustment rules, and its own course listing warns that "without adjustment, 75% of the strangle will end up in loss" — i.e., even sellers' own marketing concedes the bare strategy loses most of the time unmanaged — [Udemy course listing](https://www.udemy.com/course/nifty-option-selling-monthly-and-weekly/)

### Inferences
- The Indian options-selling "education" market is structured around tools and generic strategy taxonomies (Sensibull, Quantsapp, Zerodha Varsity) rather than codified, backtested systems with disclosed rules — closer to a "how options work" layer than a US-style published systematic framework (e.g., tastytrade's documented mechanical rules).

## What is the current state of NSE weekly/monthly expiry structure for Nifty and Bank Nifty as of 2026, and how has SEBI's 2024–2025 regulation changed the options-selling landscape?

### Takeaway
SEBI's late-2024 through late-2025 reforms materially reshaped the Indian index options market: weekly expiries were cut to one benchmark index per exchange (Nifty for NSE, killing Bank Nifty/FinNifty/Midcap weeklies), minimum contract values and lot sizes roughly tripled, premium must now be paid upfront, and intraday (not just end-of-day) position-limit monitoring was introduced through 2025. These changes raise capital requirements and transaction costs per trade and have already shifted global ranking of the most-traded contract from Bank Nifty to Nifty options.

### Cited Findings
- SEBI's core 6-measure F&O package took effect from 20 November 2024: (1) minimum contract value raised from ~₹5 lakh to ₹15–20 lakh at introduction; (2) only one weekly-expiry benchmark index per exchange (NSE kept Nifty 50; Bank Nifty, Nifty Financial Services, and Nifty Midcap Select weeklies discontinued; BSE kept Sensex); (3) a 2% extra Extreme Loss Margin on short index options positions on expiry day; (4) intraday monitoring of position limits — [Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/markets/news/sebi-announces-six-key-changes-to-curb-speculation-in-derivatives-trading-124100101316_1.html), [Zerodha bulletin](https://zerodha.com/z-connect/?p=419444)
- Upfront premium payment by option buyers became mandatory from 1 February 2025; calendar-spread margin benefit on expiry day was removed from 10 February 2025 — [Liquide blog](https://blog.liquide.life/sebi-new-fo-regulations-november-2024/)
- The last Bank Nifty weekly options expiry was 13 November 2024; NSE's CEO Ashishkumar Chauhan publicly said overall index-options volume would fall meaningfully and that "some of the volumes will disappear" rather than migrate to Bank Nifty monthlies — [5paisa](https://5paisa.com/news/sebi-directive-to-end-bank-nifty-weekly-options-impact-on-nse-volumes), [Outlook Business](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/markets/will-have-to-wait-and-see-impact-on-business-nses-sriram-krishnan-on-derivative-curbs)
- Bank Nifty weeklies had represented almost half of NSE's weekly-options premium turnover in April–September 2024 before being discontinued, and ~90% of index-options trading volume was concentrated on expiry day itself (the cited rationale for SEBI's move) — [Bajaj Broking](https://www.bajajbroking.in/blog/nse-to-end-weekly-expiry-for-bank-nifty-fin-nifty-and-midcap)
- Lot sizes (as of January 2026, per secondary sources — not independently confirmed against an NSE circular in this pass): Nifty 50 = 65 units (revised from 75; this matches the backtester's own `lot_size=65` constant), Bank Nifty = 30 units (revised from 35, effective with the January 2026 monthly contract expiring 27 Jan 2026) — [Algotest](https://algotest.in/blog/nifty-lot-size.md), [Zerodha Market Intel bulletin](https://zerodha.com/marketintel/bulletin/429705/revision-in-lot-size-of-index-derivative-contracts-from-december-30-2025)
- NSE's weekly and monthly expiry day moved from Thursday to Tuesday starting 2 September 2025 (consistent with this repo's own `_TUESDAY_CUTOVER` constant); Bank Nifty, FinNifty, and Midcap Nifty now trade monthly contracts only (last Tuesday of month, or prior trading day if a holiday) — [onetradejournal](https://onetradejournal.com/fno-lot-size-expiry-calendar), general corroboration across multiple broker blogs
- SEBI intraday position-limit monitoring rolled out in two phases: Phase 1 (circular dated 28 March 2025, effective 1 April 2025) required exchanges to monitor existing limits intraday via at least 4 snapshots/day, explicitly with **no penalty** for intraday breaches during this phase; Phase 2 (circular SEBI/HO/MRD/TPD/CIR/P/2025/122, dated 1 September 2025) set a new entity-level net intraday limit of ₹5,000 crore (up from the prior ₹1,500 crore end-of-day limit) and gross limit of ₹10,000 crore for index options specifically, effective 1 October 2025, with an expiry-day-penalty provision effective 6 December 2025 — [SCC Online](https://www.scconline.com/blog/post/2025/09/03/sebi-equity-derivatives-intraday-limits-2025/), [Taxmann](https://www.taxmann.com/post/blog/sebi-mandates-intraday-monitoring-of-index-derivative-position-limits/), [Outlook Money](https://www.outlookmoney.com/invest/sebi-outlines-new-framework-for-monitoring-of-intraday-position-limits-for-index-derivatives)
- Global ranking shift: Bank Nifty index options were the single most-traded equity index derivative contract globally in 2023 (~39 billion contracts, per FIA/Statista data) and still held that rank in June 2024 (4.208 billion contracts that month, +65.6% YoY); by late 2024, after Bank Nifty weekly discontinuation, Nifty 50 options reportedly overtook Bank Nifty for the top global spot — the late-2024 claim comes from a lower-confidence secondary/AI-summarized source (Google Finance snippet citing Wikipedia) and was not independently confirmed against a primary FIA/WFE report — [Statista via search summary](https://statista.com/statistics/380482/leading-equity-index-futures-options-by-volume), [FOW](https://fow.com/insights/3701950-analysis-indian-equity-index-options-go-from-strength-to-strength)

### Inferences
- The combined effect of fewer weekly expiries (only Nifty weeklies survive on NSE), ~3x higher minimum contract value, higher lot sizes, and intraday position monitoring is to raise the minimum capital and operational sophistication needed to run a systematic Nifty/Bank Nifty options-selling book in India — this directly favors well-capitalized/institutional players over small discretionary retail sellers, which appears to be SEBI's intent (reducing retail speculative losses).
- Because Bank Nifty no longer has a weekly expiry, any systematic strategy (like this backtester's) that assumes weekly Bank Nifty optionality no longer reflects the current tradable instrument set — only Nifty has weeklies going forward; Bank Nifty is monthly-only. This is a material structural change post-Nov 2024 that any post-2024 Bank Nifty weekly backtest would need to account for.

### Gaps
- Could not find NSE's own primary circular for the January 2026 lot-size revision — all lot-size figures (65/30) are from secondary broker-blog sources, consistent with but not independently verified against this repo's own confirmed constant.
- Could not confirm whether the Phase 2 September 2025 intraday-limit penalty regime (effective Dec 2025) has actually begun issuing penalties, or what effect it has had on expiry-day liquidity/volume in practice (too recent for retrospective reporting at research time).
- No volume/turnover data was found for the period after the Bank Nifty weekly discontinuation (i.e., actual realized volume decline, as opposed to the CEO's pre-change prediction).

## What are the key structural/liquidity differences between Nifty/Bank Nifty options and US index options (SPX/SPY) that matter for a systematic options-selling strategy?

### Takeaway
Nifty/Bank Nifty options share SPX's core structural advantage (European-style, cash-settled, no early-assignment risk) but diverge sharply on cost structure: India has no fixed circuit/price-limit on options (only dynamic, delta-based price bands with automatic order rejection outside the band), a 2024 STT hike that specifically taxes the sell side of options premium (not found as a comparable structural feature of US options), and no standalone, liquid India VIX derivative for direct tail hedging — only India VIX futures (launched 2014) exist, and no evidence of India VIX options being listed at all.
### Cited Findings
- **Settlement/exercise style**: Nifty options are cash-settled; in-the-money options settle automatically to intrinsic value and OTM options expire worthless — functionally similar to SPX's cash-settled, European-style mechanics (sources did not use the term "European style" explicitly, so this equivalence is inferred, not quoted verbatim) — [Religare Broking](https://www.religareonline.com/blog/option-expiry-and-settlement-in-india)
- **Settlement price calculation method is disputed between sources**: one source states the final settlement price is the average Nifty 50 index value over the last 30 minutes of trading (3:00–3:30 PM IST); another (Multibagg) instead describes a closing-auction/closing-index-value mechanism — these sources conflict and the correct current method should be verified against an NSE circular, not inferred from either — [Share.Market](https://www.share.market/buzz/insights/weekly-expiry-days-in-indian-fo-markets/), [Multibagg](https://www.multibagg.ai/market-pulse/articles/nifty-options-expiry-tuesday-sideways-cmsy7ms1f00gw0zmzcepmle1f)
- **No fixed circuit filter on F&O contracts**: options and futures have no fixed daily upper/lower circuit limit; instead they have a daily **dynamic price band** — for index/stock options this band is contract-specific, computed from the option's delta and updated daily; orders outside the band are rejected at the exchange level (this is reported as 2017-era forum knowledge, so current parameters should be reconfirmed) — [TradingQnA](https://tradingqna.com/t/are-there-daily-dynamic-price-bands-for-futures-and-options/18973)
- The dynamic band can "flex" — i.e., widen by about 5% increments in the direction of movement when price approaches the edge of the band, subject to a minimum count of unique-client-code trades near the limit — [Chittorgarh/TradingQnA summary](https://tradingqna.com/t/are-there-daily-dynamic-price-bands-for-futures-and-options/18973)
- **Liquidity concentration**: Nifty and Bank Nifty ATM/near-ATM strikes carry the bulk of volume and tight spreads (illustrative example: a liquid ATM Nifty option may trade at ~₹1 or less bid-ask spread), while far-OTM/illiquid strikes can show spreads of ₹1.50+ on a ₹2–3.5 quote — this is anecdotal broker-blog guidance, not measured tick data — [broker option-chain guide, cited in search summary]
- No rigorous, current quantitative comparison of Nifty vs. SPX bid-ask spreads by strike/moneyness was found; the closest comparison point located was a 2016 US-only tastylive study comparing SPY (not SPX) spreads across ITM/ATM/OTM buckets, finding SPY tighter than the broader options market at every strike — not usable as a direct India-vs-US comparison — [tastylive](https://tastylive.com/shows/market-measures/episodes/bid-ask-spreads-the-importance-of-liquidity-04-05-2016)
- **STT on options — sell-side specific**: effective 1 October 2024, Securities Transaction Tax on the sale of options (on premium) rose from 0.0625% to 0.1%; STT on sale of futures (on contract value/turnover) rose from 0.0125% to 0.02% — this is a transaction cost charged specifically on the option-selling (writing) leg, not on the buy side, and has no direct SPX/US equivalent (the US has no equivalent federal STT on options premiums) — [Business Standard Budget 2024 coverage](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/budget/news/budget-2024-steep-stt-increase-to-tame-retail-frenzy-in-derivatives-market-124072301145_1.html)
- A further STT increase was reported for the 2026-27 Budget, effective 1 April 2026, raising futures-sale STT to 0.05% and options-premium STT to 0.15% — [Outlook Money](https://www.outlookmoney.com/amp/story/invest/stt-hike-from-april-1-2026-budget-what-it-means-for-futures-and-options-traders) (this is the most current rate found; given today's date of 22 Oct 2026, this rate should now be in effect, but was not independently cross-confirmed against a primary government notification in this pass)
- One broker CEO estimate: Zerodha's own STT collection was ~₹1,500 crore pre-hike, projected to rise to ~₹2,500 crore post-hike assuming flat volumes — a single broker's internal estimate, not an industry-wide verified figure — [m.Stock/Business Standard coverage summary]
- **India VIX derivatives**: NSE launched India VIX **futures** on 26 February 2014, with weekly contracts expiring every Tuesday at launch, open to all F&O-segment-permitted participants. The underlying India VIX index itself is computed from the best bid-ask of OTM near- and mid-month Nifty options — [Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/article/pti-stories/nse-to-float-futures-based-on-india-vix-from-feb-26-114020701257_1.html)
- No search result confirmed India VIX **options** are, or ever were, listed on NSE — searches turned up only futures-related and index-level (spot VIX value) reporting; this should be treated as an unresolved gap rather than confirmed non-existence, though the absence of any mention across multiple searches is itself informative
- India's most-traded index options (Bank Nifty historically, Nifty currently) rank among the top few most-traded equity index derivative contracts globally by contract count, per FIA/WFE-sourced data cited above — relevant context for liquidity depth at the index level (though this says little about per-strike liquidity away from ATM)

### Inferences
- The lack of any confirmed India VIX options or other listed tail-hedging volatility instrument means Indian systematic index-options sellers have no direct, exchange-listed way to hedge "vol of vol"/tail risk the way a US trader can partially use VIX options/futures or SPX put spreads funded by VIX calls — any tail hedge in India must be constructed synthetically from far-OTM Nifty/Bank Nifty puts themselves, which are also the instruments most exposed to the liquidity/spread problems noted above and are the first to become essentially untradeable (wide/frozen dynamic bands) exactly when a hedge is most needed (fast, large moves).
- The sell-side-specific STT hike (0.0625%→0.1% in 2024, apparently →0.15% from April 2026) is a structural cost that specifically penalizes the options-selling/writing side of a trade more than the buying side proportionally, which is a meaningful, India-specific drag on a high-frequency systematic selling strategy (e.g., weekly/monthly rolls) that has no direct SPX equivalent, since the US has no comparable options-premium transaction tax.
- The dynamic, delta-based price band (vs. a simple percentage circuit) combined with automatic order rejection outside the band could, in a fast-moving/gap event, make it structurally harder to exit or adjust naked short positions in India than in the US where SPX options don't face an exchange-enforced band mechanism in the same way — this is a plausible, not confirmed, inference given sparse data on how the band behaves during real stress events.

### Gaps
- No primary-source (NSE circular) confirmation of current (2026) dynamic price-band parameters, delta-based computation details, or whether/how they changed since the 2017-era forum description used above.
- No quantitative, current (2025/2026) bid-ask-spread data for Nifty or Bank Nifty options at varying strikes/moneyness was found, nor any direct, rigorous Nifty-vs-SPX spread comparison study — this is a significant evidentiary gap for anyone wanting to model realistic fill slippage.
- Could not confirm or deny the existence of India VIX options (as opposed to futures) on NSE — flagged explicitly as unresolved, not as "confirmed absent."
- Could not independently verify the April 2026 STT rate hike (futures 0.05%, options premium 0.15%) against a primary Finance Bill/government notification — sourced only from a secondary financial-media article.
- No current data on the precise current 2% Extreme Loss Margin interaction with existing SPAN/margin requirements for short index-option sellers specifically (i.e., how much higher total margin a naked short Nifty option now requires vs. pre-Nov-2024).

## What do practitioners/reports say about typical failure modes specific to Indian index options sellers?

### Takeaway
Direct, quantified Indian-market reporting on specific failure events (e.g., precise seller losses during the March 2020 COVID crash) could not be found despite multiple search attempts; however, SEBI's own multi-year study data provides strong, well-sourced quantitative evidence that the overwhelming majority of individual F&O traders in India lose money persistently, and general risk-mechanics sources confirm the structural vulnerability (margin calls, forced RMS square-offs, volatility spikes hurting naked sellers) without event-specific numbers.

### Cited Findings
- SEBI's study covering FY22–FY24 (published September 2024): ~93% of individual F&O traders incurred losses, with aggregate losses exceeding ₹1.8 lakh crore over the three years; only ~1% of individual traders earned more than ₹1 lakh net of transaction costs; loss-making traders' transaction costs amounted to ~27% of their gross losses — [Moneylife](https://www.moneylife.in/article/93-percentage-of-individual-traders-lost-rs18-lakh-crore-in-equity-fo-in-past-3-years-sebi/75210.html), [Benzinga India](https://in.benzinga.com/24/09/40994647/sebi-study-unveils-93-of-individual-f-o-traders-suffered-losses-between-fy22-and-fy24)
- SEBI's FY25 follow-up study (July 2025): >91% of individual traders in equity derivatives lost money (broadly unchanged from the prior study); net losses rose to ₹105,603 crore (up from ₹74,812 crore in FY24) — [Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/markets/news/net-losses-of-traders-in-fo-widens-in-fy25-sebi-study-125070701221_1.html)
- A later-period figure (FY26, as cited by a secondary report on SEBI data) shows nearly 88% of individual **options** traders lost money, versus ~66% of futures traders, with aggregate individual-trader net losses actually declining ~18% YoY to ₹91,685 crore (from ₹1.12 lakh crore in FY25) — plausibly reflecting the SEBI measures' intended effect of shrinking retail speculative participation; traders under 30 showed the highest loss rate of any age cohort (~89%) — this figure is from a secondary aggregator citing SEBI data, and the primary SEBI document was not directly located — [search-summarized secondary reporting, primary SEBI source not confirmed]
- An earlier SEBI baseline study (reported January 2023) found 89% of individual F&O traders incurred losses, averaging ₹1.1 lakh loss per trader in FY22 — [Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/article/markets/sebi-study-suggests-89-retail-traders-in-equity-f-o-suffered-losses-123012501466_1.html)
- Mechanically, sources confirm naked/under-margined short option positions in India face margin calls and automatic RMS (Risk Management System) square-off by the broker if additional funds aren't posted in time; failure to meet a margin call can also trigger forced liquidation of other portfolio holdings — [TradingQnA](https://tradingqna.com/t/what-if-hedge-is-sold-in-option-selling/86957), [Religare Online](https://www.religareonline.com/knowledge-centre/derivatives/what-are-naked-options-in-indian-financial-market/)
- The March 2020 COVID crash saw the Nifty fall ~38% from its February high to its March low over ~33 trading sessions, including a 10% lower-circuit hit on the Sensex on 13 March 2020 and a ~13.15% single-day Sensex fall on 23 March 2020 — confirmed market-level facts, but no source quantified option-seller-specific losses or margin-call volumes during this period despite a dedicated search — [onetradejournal](https://onetradejournal.com/glossary/market-crash), [Financial Express (Bangladesh)](https://thefinancialexpress.com.bd/stock/global/indian-stock-market-crashes-on-travel-ban-visa-suspensions-1584003177)
- General (non-event-specific) commentary confirms the structural mechanism of the failure mode: "for F&O traders, [high volatility/recession conditions mean] higher volatility and wider premiums, which helps option buyers but punishes naked option sellers through margin spikes and gaps" — generic risk-education framing, not a measured study — [search-summarized broker/educational source]

### Inferences
- The persistent ~88–93% individual-trader loss rate across multiple independent SEBI study periods (FY22 through FY26) is the single most robust, India-specific empirical fact about retail participation in this space — it should be treated as strong evidence that survivorship bias in "guru"/educator claims (gurus who show only winning periods or selectively-disclosed track records) is a major real risk, consistent with the P R Sundar SEBI enforcement action noted above.
- The absence of event-specific (COVID, budget-day, RBI-day) quantified option-seller loss data in available sources is itself a notable gap — it suggests this kind of granular forced-unwind reporting is not well covered in mainstream Indian financial media (as opposed to broad market-crash narrative coverage, which is abundant) and would likely require NSE/SEBI internal risk data or broker-specific disclosures not available via general web search.

### Gaps
- Could not find any report quantifying actual option-seller losses, broker margin-call volumes, or forced square-off counts during the March 2020 COVID crash specifically, nor during a specific Budget-day or RBI-day event, despite dedicated searching — this is an open question that would likely require searching NSE/SEBI post-event risk reports or specific financial-media deep-dives (e.g., a dated search for "margin call Nifty [specific date]") rather than general terms.
- Could not locate the primary SEBI document underlying the FY26 figures (88% options-trader loss rate) — only secondary aggregator reporting was found; the primary SEBI press release/study should be sourced directly for verification.
- No practitioner-level post-mortem (e.g., a trading-forum or blog account) of a specific Indian systematic options seller being wiped out by a specific named event was found in this pass.

## Is there any publicly available long-term track record, backtest, or study on Nifty/Bank Nifty options-selling strategies and their historical returns/drawdowns?

### Takeaway
The only academic-style source found is a University of Vaasa thesis testing delta-neutral long and short straddles on Nifty 50, which found **negative Sharpe ratios for both strategies** (i.e., no evidence of a risk-adjusted edge from short-straddle selling), with the authors themselves flagging a limited data sample. All other performance claims located (a 2.06 Sharpe/4.3% max-drawdown weekly-selling claim, and the repeated claim that 75% of unmanaged short strangles lose) are self-published, unaudited practitioner claims that should not be treated as verified.

### Cited Findings
- University of Vaasa (academic thesis): tested long and short delta-neutral straddles on Nifty 50; found both strategies produce **negative Sharpe ratios**, implying they do not generate a risk-adjusted edge; weekly short straddles showed a more consistent (but still not clearly profitable) pattern than monthly, more-volatile-period straddles, with theta decay observed in both timeframes; the author explicitly notes the study's data sample is limited — [University of Vaasa repository](https://osuva.uwasa.fi/handle/11111/19466) (thesis itself not directly fetched/read in full in this pass — summary is from search-result synthesis of the abstract/findings, not a direct read of the full text)
- An unverified, self-published LinkedIn claim describes a weekly Nifty option-selling strategy built from raw NSE bhavcopy data (starting 11 February 2019) reporting a 2.06 Sharpe ratio and 4.3% max drawdown net of costs, including an out-of-sample/holdout claim — flagged explicitly as an unverifiable personal claim with no independent review — [search-summarized LinkedIn source, URL not separately captured]
- A Zerodha newsletter (Aftermarket Report) argues that path-dependency matters: the same set of trades can produce very different drawdown profiles depending on trade ordering/sequencing, implying that a single backtest's headline drawdown figure can understate true risk — [Zerodha Aftermarket Report](https://aftermarketreport.zerodha.com/p/finally-some-relief-as-nifty-ends)
- AlgoTest (an Indian options-backtesting platform) explicitly warns that simulated backtests commonly ignore brokerage, taxes, and slippage, which makes simulated P&L look better than live P&L would — a direct, India-specific caution about backtest-vs-live gaps relevant to any strategy (including ones modeled on Black-Scholes proxies, as this repo's own CLAUDE.md gotchas acknowledge for pre-2019/real-chain pricing) — [AlgoTest blog](https://algotest.in/blog/how-to-backtest-a-strategy-on-an-option-simulator.md)

### Inferences
- The one academic-style source located directly contradicts the implicit promise of most retail options-selling education/marketing in India: it found no risk-adjusted edge (negative Sharpe) for short straddles on Nifty, which is consistent with the SEBI-study finding that the vast majority of individual F&O traders lose money, and should be weighed heavily against unaudited practitioner claims of high Sharpe/low drawdown.
