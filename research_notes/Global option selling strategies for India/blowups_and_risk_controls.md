# Short-Vol / Options-Selling Blow-Ups and Risk-Management Lessons

## What exactly happened in the February 2018 "Volmageddon" — mechanical causes and lessons

### Takeaway
Volmageddon was a mechanical feedback loop: inverse/leveraged VIX ETNs (XIV, SVXY) had to buy huge quantities of VIX futures into a spike to rebalance, which pushed VIX futures higher and forced even more buying, doubling the VIX in a day, wiping out XIV (-97%, ~$2bn) and nearly destroying short-vol fund manager LJM Partners (-80%+, assets $812M→$14M in a month). The consensus lesson is that short-gamma/short-vega products with daily rebalancing requirements can create self-reinforcing, correlated blow-up risk that dwarfs normal VaR estimates, and that prime brokers retain broad contractual discretion to force-liquidate in a crisis.

### Cited Findings
- On Feb. 5, 2018 the Cboe Volatility Index jumped a record ~20 points intraday/close-to-close, ending a long low-volatility regime; VIX futures-linked products were primary transmission points for the shock — [Bloomberg, "The Day The Vix Doubled: Tales of 'Volmageddon'"](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2019-02-06/the-day-the-vix-doubled-tales-of-volmageddon)
- XIV (Credit Suisse's inverse VIX short-term ETN) lost roughly 97% of its value (~$2 billion) in a single day on Feb 5, 2018; Credit Suisse announced redemption of all outstanding XIV shares at the Closing Indicative Value effective Feb 15, 2018 — [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2019-02-06/the-day-the-vix-doubled-tales-of-volmageddon)
- LJM Partners (Chicago, run by Anthony Caine) held $812 million in assets at the start of February 2018; by month-end that had fallen to $14 million. Funds run by LJM and an affiliate lost 80%+ — [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2019-02-06/the-day-the-vix-doubled-tales-of-volmageddon)
- LJM sued Wells Fargo alleging it was forced to sell holdings at depressed prices during the margin call; a federal judge found the Wells Fargo-LJM contracts gave the bank "broad authority" to direct trading in a stressed market, and blocked LJM's suit (Wells Fargo's own suit against LJM continued) — [BusinessInsurance.com, "Judge blocks Chicago fund LJM Partners' lawsuit against Wells Fargo"](https://www.businessinsurance.com/judge-blocks-chicago-fund-ljm-partners-lawsuit-against-wells-fargo/)
- Investors separately sued LJM's Caine and a portfolio manager alleging inadequate risk disclosures — [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2019-02-06/the-day-the-vix-doubled-tales-of-volmageddon)
- Mechanism: leveraged/inverse VIX ETPs must buy VIX futures on days VIX is up and sell on days it is down to maintain constant leverage — a "short gamma" effect where the larger the move, the more they must trade; inverse products also had to buy futures to cover losses on existing short positions, so both leveraged-long and leveraged-inverse funds ended up buying on the same side simultaneously — [CFA Institute Financial Analysts Journal, "Volmageddon and the Failure of Short-Volatility Products"](https://rpc.cfainstitute.org/research/financial-analysts-journal/2021/volmageddon-failure-short-volatility-products)
- The forced rebalancing was timed just before the funds' 16:15 NAV calculation, so the whole rebalance was compressed into a narrow post-close window — [CFA Institute FAJ summary via search](https://rpc.cfainstitute.org/research/financial-analysts-journal/2021/volmageddon-failure-short-volatility-products)
- Feedback loop: traders front-ran the rebalance by bidding up VIX futures around 15:30; because the required rebalance size is itself a function of the futures price move, the price increase forced an even larger mechanical purchase, which pushed prices higher still. One-minute futures volume at 16:08 reached roughly a quarter of the entire futures market's contracts at inflated prices; one strategist estimated the funds were forced to buy ~42,400 VIX futures that day — the largest rebalance on record. VIX moved from ~18 at the open to over 37 at the close — [CFA Institute FAJ via search summary](https://rpc.cfainstitute.org/research/financial-analysts-journal/2021/volmageddon-failure-short-volatility-products)
- A BIS note specifically analyzes the role of exchange-traded volatility products in the Feb 5, 2018 turbulence — [BIS, "Equity market turbulence of 5 February: The role of exchange-traded volatility products"](https://www.bis.org/publications/equity-market-turbulence-5-february-role-exchange-traded-volatility-products)
- Academic modeling (Krishnan and Bennington, cited in the FAJ coverage) treats the short-vol ETPs as dominant agents in a mean-field feedback model, and their risk estimate lines up closely with the realized VIX futures move that day — [CFA Institute FAJ via search summary](https://rpc.cfainstitute.org/research/financial-analysts-journal/2021/volmageddon-failure-short-volatility-products)
- Growth in product size made the problem structurally worse over time: as XIV's AUM grew, its daily rebalancing notional grew proportionally, requiring larger absolute futures purchases to maintain constant exposure; low futures price levels combined with large product size produced the largest rebalances and highest vol-of-vol — [CFA Institute FAJ via search summary](https://rpc.cfainstitute.org/research/financial-analysts-journal/2021/volmageddon-failure-short-volatility-products)

### Inferences
- The LJM and XIV cases together show two distinct but related failure modes: (1) product-structural — a rules-based rebalancing ETN whose own mechanical hedging creates the shock that destroys it, and (2) discretionary-fund — a manager running short-gamma options strategies whose prime broker's margin/liquidation rights (not the manager's own stop-loss discipline) determined the actual exit price and timing. A backtester that assumes the trader controls exit execution in a crisis is likely to understate real-world losses versus what a forced, broker-directed liquidation would produce.
- The compressed, late-day timing of the ETN rebalance (right before the 16:15 NAV mark) suggests that any strategy relying on "the market will mean-revert by end of day" assumptions is vulnerable precisely when mechanical hedging flows are most concentrated near the close — relevant to any mid-session (11:00–13:00 IST) entry assumption that intraday dynamics are calm/orderly, since late-session index moves can still cascade.

### Gaps
- Could not independently verify the final/current status of the Wells Fargo–LJM litigation beyond the 2022-era report found; a dedicated docket search would be needed to confirm final resolution.
- Did not find a precise, sourced percentage loss for SVXY (ProShares) distinct from XIV in the search results, beyond a general claim that "short-volatility ETPs lost more than 90% in one day" (academic citation via search summary, not independently verified against the primary paper).

## The "Karen the Supertrader" case — naked options selling claims and the SEC fraud action

### Takeaway
Karen Bruton ("Karen the Supertrader"), publicized by Tastytrade for selling large-size index strangles, was charged by the **SEC (not CFTC)** in 2016 with fraud for allegedly concealing trading losses rather than recognizing them, and settled via consent judgment in 2018 with disgorgement + penalties and an industry bar — a documented case of a widely publicized track record turning out to rest on misrepresented performance rather than a real edge from naked options selling.

### Cited Findings
- The enforcement action against Karen Bruton and her firm Hope Advisors LLC was brought by the **SEC**, not the CFTC — no CFTC action against Bruton was found in search results — [search of CFTC.gov enforcement pages returned unrelated 2009 case](https://www.cftc.gov/PressRoom/PressReleases/pr5675-09)
- The SEC filed its civil complaint against Hope Advisors and Karen Bruton on **May 31, 2016**, in the U.S. District Court for the Northern District of Georgia (Case No. 1:16-cv-1752). The complaint alleged Hope's and Bruton's only compensation was an incentive fee, which they allegedly inflated by trading timed to lock in month-end profits while allegedly avoiding recognition of more than $50 million in losses — [SEC Litigation Release No. 24285](https://www.sec.gov/litigation/litreleases/2018/lr24285.htm)
- On **September 13, 2018**, the N.D. Georgia court entered a consent judgment against Hope Advisors and Bruton: disgorgement of **$1,237,235** and a civil penalty of **$250,000**; Bruton and Hope neither admitted nor denied the allegations — [SEC Litigation Release No. 24285](https://www.sec.gov/litigation/litreleases/2018/lr24285.htm)
- A September 2018 SEC order instituted parallel administrative (Advisers Act) proceedings against Bruton and Hope; a later decision (Release No. 5419, final December 2019) barred Bruton from associating with any broker, dealer, investment adviser or similar regulated entity, and censured Hope Advisors — [SEC Advisers Act Release materials via search](https://www.sec.gov/files/alj/aljdec/2019/ia-5419.pdf)
- Co-defendants Todd Wortman and Dawn Roberts were also named; final judgments against them were entered September 18, 2018, with Roberts permanently enjoined from future violations of Advisers Act Sections 206(2) and 206(4) — [SEC Litigation Release No. 24285](https://www.sec.gov/litigation/litreleases/2018/lr24285.htm)
- Background: Bruton's trading was publicized through Tastytrade, where she was presented as selling large-size strangles on stock indices (undefined-risk, naked options selling) — [InvestorMint, "How Karen The Supertrader Blew Up"](https://investormint.com/investing/how-karen-the-supertrader-blew-up)

### Inferences
- The case is best read as a fraud/misrepresentation case about how a track record was reported (deferred/hidden losses), not a direct regulatory finding that naked strangle-selling itself is unsound — but it is a clear illustration of survivorship-bias risk: a widely circulated "proof that undefined-risk selling works" turned out to be propped up by concealed losses rather than a verified edge.
- The relatively modest dollar amounts in the final judgment ($1.24M disgorgement + $250K penalty) versus the alleged $50M+ of unrecognized losses suggest the SEC's monetary recovery was far smaller than the scale of the underlying alleged losses — a pattern common in these cases where enforcement outcomes understate investor harm.

### Gaps
- Could not confirm from search results what, if anything, CFTC-registered entities (e.g., any CPO/CTA registration Hope Advisors may have held) were separately examined; only the SEC civil and administrative actions were found.
- Investor-level restitution details (how much of the original ~$33M+ under management, or the alleged $50M of hidden losses, was ultimately recovered by investors) were not found in the search results and should be flagged as a gap for the report.

## Other notable short-vol blow-ups — Allianz Structured Alpha, and search for Indian examples

### Takeaway
Allianz Global Investors U.S. (AGI US) ran "Structured Alpha" funds that used S&P 500 options (a short-vol options overlay with purchased tail hedges) and collapsed by more than $7 billion in Feb–March 2020 after investors allege the manager quietly stripped out the hedges meant to limit losses; this produced a landmark 2022 resolution: AGI US pled guilty to criminal securities fraud, lost its ability to advise U.S. registered funds for 10 years, and Allianz group paid roughly $6 billion in combined criminal fines, restitution and forfeiture — one of the largest fraud settlements tied to a short-vol options strategy. No documented large-scale Indian options-seller blow-up case (as opposed to aggregate retail-loss statistics) was found in open search; this is flagged as a gap rather than an absence of risk.

### Cited Findings
- Structured Alpha funds had more than $11 billion of assets under management before the collapse and lost more than $7 billion as COVID-19 roiled markets in February–March 2020 — [Reuters-sourced reporting via search, Institutional Investor / Law360 coverage](https://www.institutionalinvestor.com/article/b1mt7hvffkyw84/Allianz-s-Volatility-Fund-Problems-Get-Bigger)
- Investors alleged the funds imploded after hedges designed to minimize losses were quietly removed; one investor suit characterized the fund's late-winter 2020 positioning as "AllianzGI's reckless throw of the dice," leaving investors with losses above 75% and alleging the manager never adequately rebalanced or bought meaningful hedges — [Reuters coverage via search; Law360, "Allianz Investment Arm Sued Over $1B Loss From Virus Crash"](https://www.law360.com/classaction/articles/1306887)
- A federal judge allowed investor negligence/bad-faith claims against Allianz to proceed — [BusinessInsurance.com, "Allianz must face investor claims over funds' collapse, US judge rules"](https://www.businessinsurance.com/allianz-must-face-investor-lawsuit-claims-over-funds-collapse-us-judge-rules/)
- In May 2022, AGI US agreed to plead guilty to one count of criminal securities fraud; the SEC separately found AGI US violated U.S. securities laws. As a consequence of the guilty plea, AGI US is disqualified from providing advisory services to U.S.-registered investment funds for 10 years and will exit that business — [Institutional Investor, "Charged With Fraud by SEC and DOJ Allianz Settles by Paying Billions"](https://www.institutionalinvestor.com/article/b1y2lj4jkfmvzc/Charged-With-Fraud-by-SEC-and-DOJ-Allianz-Settles-by-Paying-Billions)
- Reported payment figures vary by source but the headline total is commonly reported near **$6 billion**, with roughly $5 billion earmarked for compensating Structured Alpha investors; one source breaks this down as forfeiture of $174.3 million (DOJ) plus $675 million penalty (SEC), while another cites a $2.33 billion criminal fine, $3.24 billion of restitution and a $463 million forfeiture per court documents — sources conflict on the exact breakdown though the ~$6bn headline total is consistent — [InsuranceBusinessMag, "Allianz investment arm pleads guilty to securities fraud"](https://www.insurancebusinessmag.com/uk/news/breaking-news/allianz-investment-arm-pleads-guilty-to-securities-fraud-406337.aspx); [InstitutionalInvestor](https://www.institutionalinvestor.com/article/b1y2lj4jkfmvzc/Charged-With-Fraud-by-SEC-and-DOJ-Allianz-Settles-by-Paying-Billions)
- The SEC's complaint alleged the strategy's lead portfolio manager, Gregoire Tournant, "orchestrated the multi-year scheme to mislead investors"; Tournant and other portfolio managers faced parallel criminal proceedings; Allianz stated the criminal misconduct was limited to "a handful of individuals" in AGI US's Structured Products Group who are no longer with the company, and that its investigation found no participation by Allianz SE (the German parent) — [InsuranceBusinessMag](https://www.insurancebusinessmag.com/us/news/breaking-news/allianz-investment-arm-pleads-guilty-to-fraud-in-hedge-funds-scandal-406336.aspx)
- As part of restructuring after the scandal, Allianz Global announced a deal transferring its U.S. investment teams and assets to Voya Investment Management in exchange for up to a 24% equity stake in Voya IM — [search summary of Institutional Investor / DGAP coverage](https://www.boerse-muenchen.de/news/EQS179383/DGAP-Adhoc_-Allianz-SE-announces-resolution-of-U.S.-governmental-investigations-concerning-Structured-Alpha-with-a-guilty-plea-by-AGI-US.-Allianz-SE-also-announces-signing-of-a-MoU-for-a-new-long-term)
- Indian market context (aggregate, not a single blow-up case): a SEBI study found individual F&O traders lost about ₹91,685 crore in equity F&O in FY26 (down from ~₹1.12 lakh crore the prior year), with options making up roughly 92% of those aggregate losses; the same study found about 97% of individual traders were primarily options *buyers*, and only about 2% were primarily *sellers* — [Moneylife, "92% of Aggregate Losses Incurred by Individuals Are from Options Trading: SEBI Study"](https://www.moneylife.in/article/92-percentage-of-aggregate-losses-incurred-by-individuals-are-from-options-trading-sebi-study/81429.html); [Free Press Journal, "89% of young F&O traders lose money, finds SEBI study"](https://www.freepressjournal.in/business/89-of-young-fo-traders-lose-money-finds-sebi-study)
- 59% of index-options turnover in FY26 still occurred on expiry day per the same SEBI-sourced reporting, concentrating gamma/pin risk around weekly/monthly expiry — [Free Press Journal](https://www.freepressjournal.in/business/89-of-young-fo-traders-lose-money-finds-sebi-study)
- The one large, crore-scale Nifty-options loss event found in search was **not** a short-vol strategy blow-up but a June 2022 "fat-finger" mis-trade: Vardhaman Global reportedly lost Rs 200–250 crore after erroneously selling 25,000 lots of Nifty call options at the 14,500 strike at a very low price — [Moneylife, "Fat Finger Trade in Nifty Options on Thursday Leads to a Loss of Rs200 Crore-Rs250 Crore"](https://www.moneylife.in/article/fat-finger-trade-in-nifty-options-on-thursday-leads-to-a-loss-of-rs200-rs250-crore/67385.html)

### Inferences
- The Allianz case is structurally the most relevant global precedent for a systematic-selling backtester: it was a *defined, hedged* short-vol overlay on paper, but the documented failure mode was that hedges were discretionarily reduced/removed right before the tail event — i.e., the blow-up came from a governance/compliance failure to maintain the stated hedge ratio, not from the short-vol premise itself being unhedgeable. This directly supports a lesson that hedge-ratio enforcement needs to be mechanical/auditable, not left to manager discretion, especially under drawdown pressure when the temptation to "size down the hedge to preserve P&L" is highest.
- No documented, named, large-scale Indian proprietary short-vol fund or well-known individual "option seller" blow-up (analogous to LJM or Allianz) surfaced in open search; Indian commentary instead emphasizes aggregate retail options-buyer losses (not seller blow-ups) and expiry-day concentration risk. This is a meaningful asymmetry: the Indian options narrative in financial media is dominated by retail buyers losing small amounts repeatedly, not sellers blowing up in single catastrophic events — though the small share of sellers (~2% of individual traders) and lack of detailed loss attribution by trader type means a large individual seller blow-up could exist without being separately reported.

### Gaps
- No specific, named Indian large options-seller blow-up case (analogous to LJM/Allianz) was found despite targeted searches using terms like "option seller loss India," "naked options selling blow up India," and "Nifty/Bank Nifty option seller wiped out margin call." This may reflect (a) genuine scarcity of such large, publicly reported cases, (b) under-reporting of individual trader losses relative to institutional ones, or (c) search terms not matching how Indian financial press frames these stories (e.g., under trader-suicide or SEBI-circular coverage) — a follow-up search specifically on Moneycontrol/ET/LiveMint site search with trader names or specific dates would be needed to close this gap definitively.
- Did not find other named short-vol blow-ups explicitly requested (e.g., a distinct "1Earth" fund) in search results — no evidence surfaced either confirming or describing such a fund; flagged as unresearched/unconfirmed rather than nonexistent.

## Risk-management techniques credited by surviving/successful systematic options sellers

### Takeaway
Across the practitioner and limited academic sources found, the recurring prescriptions are: (1) use defined-risk structures (spreads/iron condors) sized materially smaller than undefined-risk (naked strangle) positions; (2) cap undefined-risk buying-power usage at roughly 3–7% of account value per position (vs. 1–3% for defined-risk); (3) reduce size or halt new short-vol entries as VIX/India VIX rises past threshold bands; (4) maintain purchased tail hedges (far-OTM puts or VIX calls) as a structural overlay, accepting an ongoing cost (one estimate ~3.6%/year) as insurance; and (5) treat correlation spikes during stress as a reason to cut gross exposure, since a seemingly diversified book of short-vol positions can collapse into one effectively concentrated position when correlations go to 1.

### Cited Findings
- Tastylive (Jim Schultz) guidance: size defined-risk positions at roughly 1%–3% of total account value, and undefined-risk positions at roughly 3%–7%, noting smaller accounts may need to deviate — [Tastylive, "Defined Risk and Undefined Risk Position Sizing"](https://www.tastylive.com/news-insights/Defined-Risk-and-Undefined-Risk-Position-Sizing)
- A related Tastylive piece argues buying-power requirement (BPR) is a usable proxy for a position's probable maximum loss, since BPR tends to correspond to roughly a 5% likelihood of a loss reaching that size, and recommends keeping undefined-risk positions near 5% of account value as "the best thing you can do" to avoid blowing up an account — noting BPR can still understate true losses in some scenarios — [Tastylive, "Defined Risk Strategies: Avoid Blowing Up Your Account"](https://www.tastylive.com/news-insights/Defined-Risk-Strategies-Avoid-Blowing-Up-Your-Account)
- A short strangle is "undefined risk" because its theoretical maximum loss is unbounded; adding further OTM long options (converting to an iron condor/defined-risk structure) caps the loss but reduces collected premium — in one worked example credit fell from $122 to $69 when $5-wide wings were added, while margin fell to ~$435, raising the computed return-on-margin to 15.86% — [Optionalpha backtest summary via search](https://optionalpha.com/unlimited-risk-option-strategies-102250.html)
- Trade-off acknowledged even by proponents of undefined risk: Optionalpha's own backtests found short straddles/strangles produced the strongest raw returns versus defined-risk versions, but iron condors can match a strangle's margin requirement while collecting more premium than a plain spread — at the cost of narrower breakevens and less cushion against adverse moves — [Optionalpha via search summary](https://optionalpha.com/unlimited-risk-option-strategies-102250.html)
- VIX-based size scaling (discretionary, non-academic sources): one approach cuts position size roughly in half once VIX exceeds 25 and to about a third above 35, on the reasoning that if average true range doubles, maintaining size doubles dollar risk; another "Rule of Halves" halves size once VIX exceeds 30; a third proposes scaling adjusted risk as Base Risk × (Baseline VIX / Current VIX), treating VIX above 30 as a "high fear" regime requiring significant size/leverage cuts — [Moomoo, "How to Trade When VIX Is Above 25"](https://www.moomoo.com/us/learn/how-to-trade-high-volatility); [Day Trading Toolkit, "The Trader's Playbook: How to Day Trade in a High VIX Market"](https://daytradingtoolkit.com/strategies/traders-playbook-high-vix-market); [ThetaEdge, "Dynamic Position Sizing in Volatile Markets"](https://nextapi.thetaedge.ai/blog/dynamic-position-sizing-volatile-markets)
- India VIX-specific banding found (non-authoritative, educational source): sizing at 100% for India VIX below 13, 75% for 13–17, and 50% or less above 17; a separate source suggests halving size once India VIX crosses 20 — [TradingView educational post, "How to Use India VIX to Size Your Positions"](https://de.tradingview.com/chart/NIFTY/tVtKiBKA-How-to-Use-India-VIX-to-Size-Your-Positions)
- Tail-risk hedging: deep OTM puts (delta ≈ -0.05 to -0.10) expire worthless most of the time but pay off disproportionately in tail events; one estimate puts the ongoing annual cost of a rolling OTM-put hedge at about 3.6% of notional — a cost that must be justified purely by the tail-event payoff, not by regular P&L — [Sparkco.ai, "Excel Tail Hedging with OTM Puts & Volatility Strategies"](https://sparkco.ai/blog/excel-tail-hedging-with-otm-puts-volatility-strategies)
- Puts vs. VIX calls as a hedge: put options give a more predictable, level-specific offset to a decline, while VIX-linked instruments respond directionally (and typically more sharply) to a vol spike itself, independent of the underlying's exact decline level — [Russell Investments tail-risk paper via search summary](https://russellinvestments.com/-/media/files/au/insights/r_rpt_tailrisk_v1f_1609.pdf)
- Institutional overlay design: dial hedge intensity up/down using rules-based triggers (e.g., implied volatility in its lowest historical decile, or credit spreads at cycle tights) rather than discretionary manager judgment — directly relevant given that discretionary hedge removal is the alleged root cause in the Allianz case above — [search summary, institutional tail-risk overlay source](https://resonanzcapital.com/insights/strategic-tail-risk-hedging-building-antifragility-into-institutional-portfolios)
- Correlation/concentration: a diversified-looking book of short-vol positions across assets can behave like one concentrated position once correlations converge toward 1 during stress, at which point drawdowns accelerate; simultaneous drawdowns across multiple assets are a signal of systemic (not idiosyncratic) risk and should trigger de-risking action — [practitioner blog via search summary](https://medium.com/@samirvarma/the-stop-loss-that-stops-gains-why-your-drawdown-rules-are-probably-hurting-you-4caff337916f)
- Drawdown-control design: academic work on dynamic maximum-drawdown control allocates between cash and the risky book to keep realized losses below a chosen ceiling, with variants that handle lock-up periods and liquidity constraints via a hedging overlay rather than outright deleveraging — [EDHEC-Risk working paper via search summary](https://www.cirje.e.u-tokyo.ac.jp/research/dp/2009/2009cf625.pdf) / [EDHEC-Risk publications page](https://risk.edhec.edu/publications/dynamic-allocation-strategies-absolute-and)
- Practitioner guidance explicitly argues hard risk limits (max drawdown, position caps, kill switch) should be enforced in deterministic code outside of any model or discretionary override — directly analogous to the backtester's own DD kill-switch design — [trading-skill practitioner source via search summary](https://playbooks.com/skills/omer-metin/skills-for-antigravity/risk-management-trading)
- Static drawdown/VIX thresholds are criticized as unable to adapt across regimes; percentile-based (regime-relative) thresholds are suggested as more robust, and any such rule should be backtested across multiple regimes and instruments before being trusted live — [practitioner source via search summary](https://www.acadifi.com/community/drawdown-risk-management-maximum-drawdown-recovery-time-analysis)

### Inferences
- There is a consistent, repeated numeric anchor across independent tastylive-style sources: undefined-risk (naked) positions should be capped around 3–7% of account value per position, roughly half to a third the allocation given to defined-risk spreads — this is a rule of thumb from options educators with a commercial interest in promoting their platforms, not an independently backtested optimum, and should be labeled as such in any report.
- The Allianz case strongly reinforces that *having* a hedge overlay on paper is insufficient; the differentiator between survivors and failures is whether the hedge ratio is mechanically enforced (rules-based triggers, no discretionary override) versus left to a manager's judgment under P&L pressure.
- None of the sizing/VIX-threshold rules found were independently backtested in the sources themselves (they are stated as heuristics); a systematic backtester (like this repo's) is better positioned to empirically test these specific thresholds (e.g., "halve size above VIX 25/India VIX 20") than to take them as proven.

### Gaps
- Found no CBOE-published research note specifically quantifying VIX-level-based position sizing or trading halts (searches returned only an unrelated 2006 SEC rule filing on VIX position limits) — this specific sub-claim in the brief could not be sourced and should be treated as absent from available search results, not confirmed.
- Found no rigorous, peer-reviewed study isolating kill-switch efficacy specifically for short-volatility/options-selling books (as opposed to general systematic strategies) — flagged as a gap; closing it would need SSRN/Journal of Portfolio Management searches beyond this session's scope.

## Asymmetry of short-vol payoffs and position-sizing/Kelly implications

### Takeaway
Short volatility is widely described (by both critics and some proponents) via the "picking up pennies/nickels in front of a steamroller" metaphor — frequent small gains funded by rare, severe losses — and the serious quantitative literature argues that naive full-Kelly sizing is dangerous here because Kelly's lognormal assumption ignores the fat tails, negative skew, and regime-dependent volatility-of-volatility that actually characterize option-selling P&L; the credited fix is fractional Kelly plus explicit, VIX-regime-aware size scaling, validated by at least one 2025 study comparing Kelly-only, VIX-only, and hybrid sizing on index put-writing.

### Cited Findings
- The "picking up pennies/nickels in front of a steamroller" framing is widely used for short volatility: M&G describes it as a bet that volatility stays subdued, generating steady gains in calm markets but leaving large losses exposed when volatility spikes — [M&G, short volatility note](https://mandg.co.za/insights/articlesreleases/short-volatility)
- Risk.net frames the same metaphor around the strategy's outsized downside when it fails, in coverage of hedge funds using "short volatility QIS" and claims of stripping out tail risk — [Risk.net, "Hedge funds pile into short volatility QIS options"](https://www.risk.net/markets/7960265/hedge-funds-pile-into-short-volatility-qis-options)
- Mauldin Economics uses the "nickels" variant specifically, arguing sophisticated traders understand this is literally what they are doing — [Mauldin Economics, "Odds On My D"](https://www.mauldineconomics.com/the-10th-man/odds-on-my-d.c)
- On leveraged short-vol products specifically, commentary (Jesse Felder, via Hedgeye) argues that using 2x-leveraged inverse VIX ETFs rather than a plain 1x short-vol product compounds both leverage and total risk — [Hedgeye reposting Felder Report](https://app.hedgeye.com/insights/74743-why-we-may-be-headed-for-another-volatility-event/print)
- Academic Kelly critique: a paper on "Leverage and Uncertainty" argues negative skew and excess kurtosis justify using a *fractional* Kelly bet rather than full Kelly for a single asset with fat-tailed, skewed returns — tail risk mechanically shrinks the optimal Kelly fraction — and separately notes tail parameters are inherently subjective, so different practitioners' estimates of the "right" growth-optimal size can diverge widely — [arXiv, "Leverage and Uncertainty"](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1612.07194)
- A 2025 arXiv study ("Sizing the Risk: Kelly, VIX, and Hybrid Approaches in Put-Writing on Index Options") directly compares Kelly sizing, VIX-regime-based sizing, and a hybrid of both for short-dated index put-writing; its headline findings are that ultra-short-dated, far out-of-the-money options deliver superior risk-adjusted returns, and that the **hybrid** sizing approach "consistently balances return generation with robust drawdown control" better than either pure method alone; it explicitly flags that the nonlinear payoff of options introduces fat-tailed risk exposure, so improper (i.e., naive full-Kelly or static) sizing can produce catastrophic drawdowns — [arXiv, "Sizing the Risk: Kelly, VIX, and Hybrid Approaches in Put-Writing on Index Options"](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.16598v1)
- General limits-of-Kelly critique: Kelly assumes a known return distribution (commonly lognormal with known μ/σ); real markets exhibit fat tails, skewness, and regime shifts the lognormal model does not capture, and under such conditions an operator's practical left-tail control comes mainly from derivatives/insurance (hedges) or dynamic, stop-loss-based portfolio construction rather than from static Kelly sizing alone — [arXiv, "Statistical Consequences of Fat Tails"](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2001.10488)

### Inferences
- The two academic sources together imply a two-layer risk-sizing architecture is preferable to a single Kelly number: (1) a fractional-Kelly base size discounted for known skew/kurtosis, overlaid with (2) a regime-conditional (VIX-level) multiplier — which is effectively what the 2025 hybrid-sizing paper tests and validates empirically for exactly this backtester's domain (index put-writing), making it the single most directly applicable academic source found for this project's "entry_threshold"/VIX-gate design philosophy.
- The repeated "steamroller/pennies" framing across independent financial commentators (not just critics of short-vol, but also providers describing their own tail-hedge products) suggests this asymmetry is now conventional wisdom in the industry, not a contrarian critique — meaning any report section on this topic can treat the core asymmetry claim as well-established rather than contested, while still flagging that specific sizing numbers (3-7% BPR, VIX-based halving rules) remain heuristic rather than rigorously validated outside the one 2025 paper found.
