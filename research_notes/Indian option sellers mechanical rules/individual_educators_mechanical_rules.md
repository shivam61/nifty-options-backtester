# Individual Indian Options-Selling Educators — Published Mechanical Trading Rules

Scope: PUBLISHED mechanical rules (entry timing, strike selection, sizing, stop-loss, profit booking, adjustment logic) taught by named Indian options-selling educators/traders, for Nifty/Bank Nifty short straddle/strangle with adjustments. Confidence tiers used throughout:
- **[SEBI-RIA-published]** — rule published by a SEBI-registered investment adviser/research analyst as part of registered advisory content.
- **[Educator/platform-published, no audit]** — rule stated in an educator's or platform's own course page, blog, video description, or marketing copy, with no independent audit of results.
- **[Forum-anecdote]** — a rule as paraphrased/attributed by a third party (forum post, review, secondhand summary), not the educator's own primary text — lowest confidence, may be inaccurate or outdated.

None of the rules below should be read as a verified track record. See the "backtested/live P&L disclosure" question for explicit treatment of performance claims.

## Q3: Concrete rule extraction — entry time, strike selection, sizing, stop-loss, profit booking, adjustment triggers

### Takeaway
No single named Indian educator's full concrete rule set (all six dimensions) could be verified end-to-end from primary sources in this pass. What is available is a set of **generic, platform/forum-sourced numeric heuristics** that recur across multiple unattributed or lightly-attributed Indian sources, which likely reflect what individual educators teach in substance even though exact attribution is missing.

### Cited Findings
- **Stop-loss (generic, Indian retail options-selling context):** "Keep a stop loss of double the premium on either side option" (i.e., 2x credit received) is described as "the simplest way" to manage a short straddle/strangle leg, with the caveat that "sometimes however that also may not work." **[Forum/blog-published, no audit]** — found via search of Indian iron-condor/adjustment blog content (equitiesindia.com / algotest.in cluster); exact source page: [Equities India — adjustment trading options](https://equitiesindia.com/glossary/adjustment-trading-options)
- An alternative stop-loss framing: set the stop at the point where losses equal the net credit received, "ensuring a break-even outcome at worst" (i.e., 1x credit, breakeven stop) — a more conservative variant of the 2x rule above. **[Forum/blog-published, no audit]**
- **Generic adjustment triggers** (Entri, cited above): (1) index within ~100–150 points of the sold strike, (2) sold premium doubles, (3) loss reaches a fixed limit, commonly cited as 1.5x–2x the premium collected. — [Entri](https://entri.app/explore/stock-market/adjusting-option-selling-strategies/)
- **Adjustment techniques** recurring across sources: rolling the threatened/tested strike further away; rolling the untested (safe) side closer to the money to collect more credit and re-center the position; rolling to the next expiry (time roll); adding a long hedge (far OTM option) to cap tail risk; converting the position into an iron condor/iron butterfly by buying wings, which caps max loss but reduces net credit and tightens breakevens. — [Entri](https://entri.app/explore/stock-market/adjusting-option-selling-strategies/); [Equities India — iron fly vs iron condor](https://equitiesindia.com/glossary/iron-fly-vs-iron-condor); general forum discussion on "how do you adjust your iron condors" — [TradingQnA](https://tradingqna.com/t/how-do-you-adjust-your-iron-condors-what-are-the-trigger-points-for-adjustments/108139)
- One trader/forum post specifically on rolling mechanics: "Roll down/up the untested side... I usually do it when the tested side premium is double the value" — i.e., roll-the-untested-leg trigger pegged to the tested leg's premium doubling. **[Forum-anecdote]** — found in search results for Bank Nifty/Nifty strangle adjustment forum threads (Lemonn/TradingQnA cluster); representative page: [Lemonn — Short Strangle Adjustments](https://lemonn.co.in/blog/knowledge-hub/short-strangle-adjustments/)
- **Profit booking (generic, cited by an Indian retail trader on a forum, not an educator's branded rule):** "50% of max profit (profit) or -100% of the max profit (loss) as my exit strategy for swing and positional trades for Nifty." **[Forum-anecdote]** — [TradingQnA — Exit strategy for intraday option selling in Nifty](https://tradingqna.com/t/exit-strategy-for-intraday-option-selling-in-nifty/74402)
- **Position sizing / margin context (India-specific, structural fact not a strategy "rule" per se):** a single Nifty weekly short call typically blocks roughly ₹1.0–1.4 lakh in SPAN + exposure margin, rising if the market moves against the position; lot sizes cited: Nifty 65, Bank Nifty 15, FinNifty 25, Sensex 10 (lot sizes should be cross-checked against current NSE contract specs as they are periodically revised by the exchange). **[Educator/platform-published, no audit]** — [OneTradeJournal — Top Rules for Options Sellers](https://onetradejournal.com/learn/top-rules-for-options-sellers); [OneTradeJournal — Top Option Selling Strategies](https://onetradejournal.com/learn/top-option-selling-strategies)
- **Entry timing (generic, not attributed to a specific named Indian educator):** one TradingView strategy note suggests a 9:45–10:15 AM entry window combined with India VIX below roughly 13–14 as conditions favoring a straddle-sell setup. **[Forum/platform-published, no audit, unattributed]** — found in search results for "P R Sundar short straddle adjustment rules"; representative page cluster around [TradingView — Straddle Selling in Sideways Market](https://jp.tradingview.com/chart/NIFTY/c8aCEhbi-Straddle-Selling-in-Sideways-Market-Full-Risk-Reward-Strategy)
- **Event-risk rule (generic):** convert a naked short into a defined-risk spread ahead of known binary events (Union Budget, RBI monetary policy, major index-heavyweight earnings) because the IV crush around such events cuts both ways and naked risk is asymmetric. **[Educator/platform-published, no audit]** — [OneTradeJournal — Top Rules for Options Sellers](https://onetradejournal.com/learn/top-rules-for-options-sellers)
- **Risk cap heuristic:** one guide suggests capping adjustments/rolls at two per trade as a sensible limit, and separately warns against "doubling your position to recover" a loss (i.e., no martingale sizing). **[Forum/blog-published, no audit]**

### Inferences
- There is a recognizable **numeric cluster** across independent, mostly-unattributed Indian sources: stop-loss commonly expressed as a multiple of credit received (1x = breakeven stop, 2x = "double the premium" stop being the most frequently cited figure), and adjustment/roll triggers commonly expressed either as a premium-doubling event on the tested leg or as a fixed loss multiple (1.5x–2x credit). This convergence across independently-found sources is more informative than any single source, even though none of it is attributable to a single celebrity educator's named, verbatim rule.
- Profit booking at "50% of max profit" recurs here (from an Indian forum poster) and is consistent with the general (non-Indian-specific) options-selling literature convention of closing short premium at 50% of max profit to improve win-rate/Sharpe — suggesting Indian retail educators are likely importing this convention rather than inventing a distinct one, though this inference is not directly sourced to a named Indian educator.

### Gaps
- No source gave a delta-based strike selection rule (e.g., "sell the 20-delta strike") attributed to an Indian educator specifically — Indian retail content found in this pass skews toward ATM-straddle or fixed-%-OTM framing rather than delta framing, which is more common in US-centric sources (tastylive/Option Alpha, both cited above as comparison points, not as Indian sources).
- No source quantified "how many points/% OTM" for strangle strike selection from a named Indian educator.

## Q5: Is there a common "playbook" pattern / consensus across educators?

### Takeaway
Yes — even without being able to attribute a complete, verbatim rule set to any single named celebrity educator, the research surfaced a consistent convergence across multiple **independent, mostly-unattributed** Indian retail-options sources on the shape of the playbook: sell ATM (or near-ATM) premium, size conservatively relative to margin, use a credit-multiple stop-loss (most often "2x credit" or breakeven-equivalent), book profit early (50% of max profit is the one concretely-cited figure), and manage a breached leg primarily by (a) rolling the untested/safe leg closer to re-center the trade and collect more credit, and/or (b) buying a hedge / converting to a defined-risk structure (iron condor/iron fly) once losses cross a threshold, rather than holding naked risk indefinitely. This pattern matches the general global options-selling-education convention (also seen in non-Indian sources like tastylive/Option Alpha cited for comparison) more than it reflects any India-specific innovation.

### Cited Findings
- See Q3 findings above for the specific numeric convergences (2x/1x credit-multiple stop; 1.5–2x loss-trigger for adjustment; roll-the-untested-leg-on-premium-doubling; convert-to-iron-fly-or-condor-to-cap-risk; 50%-of-max-profit booking).
- Multiple independent sources (Entri, Equities India, OneTradeJournal, assorted forum threads, Udemy course descriptions) state substantially the same adjustment menu — rolling the tested strike away, rolling the untested side closer, rolling to next expiry, adding a long hedge, converting to a capped-risk spread, or exiting outright — without citing each other, which is a form of independent convergence. — [Entri](https://entri.app/explore/stock-market/adjusting-option-selling-strategies/); [Equities India](https://equitiesindia.com/glossary/adjustment-trading-options); [OneTradeJournal](https://onetradejournal.com/learn/top-rules-for-options-sellers)
- For comparison (non-Indian, but referenced in search results as the likely template this convention derives from): tastylive's 2016 SPY study comparing short straddles to iron flies managed at 25% of max profit found that waiting for the iron fly to hit the straddle's profit target reduced win rate with little change to average P&L — i.e., even the "converted" structure's own target-booking convention (around 25-50% of max profit) is a transplant from this US-centric educational tradition. — [tastylive — Iron Flies/Straddle Profit Targets](https://www.tastylive.com/shows/market-measures/episodes/iron-flies-straddle-profit-targets-12-06-2016)

### Inferences
- The consensus playbook found here is a generic, credit-multiple-driven defensive framework (stop at 1-2x credit, roll/hedge/convert on breach, book at ~50% of max profit) rather than a precise, education-specific proprietary system — this strongly suggests that most retail Indian options-selling course content is repackaging a small number of well-known defensive heuristics (several of which trace back to US options-education sources like tastytrade/Option Alpha) rather than each educator having a genuinely distinct, independently-validated methodology.
- Because none of this is independently backtested or audited in public, the project's own backtester (`CombinedBacktestEngine`) should treat these consensus rules strictly as a reference/comparison baseline for "what retail practice claims to do," not as validated parameters — consistent with the project's existing finding (per `CLAUDE.md`) that model-priced weekly backtests already show the real-price chain engine result is NOT SUPPORTED for a weekly short-premium edge; these educator rules do not provide independent evidence to the contrary.

### Gaps
- Could not confirm whether any of the specific named educators in the task (P R Sundar, Vivek Bajaj, Ankit Gala, SpiderSoftwareIndia) are the original source of any of these specific numeric conventions, or whether they are all independently drawing from the same pre-existing (likely US-origin) options-education canon. Attribution at the level of "educator X specifically teaches Y number" remains unconfirmed for every numeric rule in this document except the two explicitly-attributed forum anecdotes (the "double the tested premium" roll trigger, and the "50% of max profit / -100% of max profit" exit rule), both of which are anonymous forum posters, not the named celebrity educators themselves.
- A follow-up pass with working access to zerodha.com (blocked by a DNS/network error on the fetch tool in this session) and to the named educators' own YouTube/website/course-PDF content directly (rather than via search-engine snippets only) is recommended to close the primary-source gap for Q1 and Q2.
