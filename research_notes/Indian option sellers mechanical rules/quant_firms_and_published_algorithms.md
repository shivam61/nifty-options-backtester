## Are there published academic papers (IIM/ISB/IIT/SSRN) specifically studying systematic Nifty/Bank Nifty options-selling strategies and their backtested performance/risk?

### Takeaway
Indian academic literature on Nifty options is dominated by older volatility-mispricing / option-pricing-efficiency studies (IIM Ahmedabad, and journal articles testing Black-Scholes fit), not by papers that backtest a systematic options-*selling* (short-premium) strategy with P&L/drawdown/Sharpe results. No ISB-specific paper was found in this pass.

### Cited Findings
- Jayanth R. Varma (IIM Ahmedabad), **"Mispricing of Volatility in the Indian Index Options Market"** (Working Paper 2002-04-01): uses Nifty index-futures prices and the Breeden-Litzenberger approach to back out risk-neutral return distributions from the implied-volatility smile; finds the implied distribution is more sharply peaked with thinner tails than normal/historical distributions in all but one case, i.e., the market *underprices* volatility/tail risk; also flags some overpricing of deep-ITM calls — [IIMA working paper page](https://www.iima.ac.in/publication/mispricing-volatility-theindian-index-options-market) / [PDF](https://web.iima.ac.in/publications/data/2002-04-01JRVarma.pdf)
- Narain, Nigam & Pandey (2016), *Journal of Advances in Management Research*: use daily CNX Nifty option data 2004–2014 and describe their work as the first decadal study confirming the volatility-smile shape persists for Indian index options — a pricing-shape study, not a trading-strategy backtest — [IDEAS/RePEc record](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eme/jamrpp/jamr-09-2015-0062.html)
- Sehgal & Vijayakumar (2009) test Black-Scholes fit on Nifty options for 2004–2005: using historical volatility, both calls and puts appear fairly priced (subject to spot-market trading asymmetry); using a weighted-implied-volatility measure, the model grossly *underestimates* option values — again a pricing-efficiency study, not a selling-strategy backtest [cited via search summary; primary source not independently fetched in this pass].

### Inferences
- The available Indian academic literature on Nifty options clusters around option-pricing efficiency / volatility-smile description (mostly pre-2016, pre-weekly-expiry era), not systematic strategy backtesting. This likely reflects (a) data access constraints for academics (no free long history of NSE option chain data, similar to the gotcha this codebase documents about Black-Scholes-proxy pricing), and (b) a research-interest lag behind the weekly-options/retail-options boom that only took off post-2019.
- The absence of any Nifty/Bank Nifty options-*selling* backtest paper in top Indian B-school repositories is a meaningful negative finding: if such a study existed with credible results, it would likely be well-indexed and discoverable (SSRN papers on strategy backtests tend to rank well for their exact subject-matter queries). Its absence suggests either (a) it doesn't exist in that form, or (b) it exists but under generic "volatility risk premium in India" framing not surfaced by these specific queries.

### Gaps
- No ISB (Indian School of Business) working paper was found in this pass; the query returned only IIM Ahmedabad results. A targeted search of ISB's own faculty/working-paper repository (not attempted here) is needed to close this gap.
- No IIT (any campus) finance-lab paper on Nifty/Bank Nifty options selling was found; this pass did not run an IIT-specific query.
- No direct SSRN abstract page was retrieved (searches surfaced only aggregator/forum content, not an actual SSRN paper); a direct SSRN full-text-search query (e.g., via SSRN's own site search) was not attempted and would be the natural next step.
- Could not independently verify the Narain/Nigam/Pandey and Sehgal/Vijayakumar findings against primary-source PDFs within the tool budget for this pass — summarized as reported by search-engine synthesis of secondary listings (IDEAS/RePEc, Christ University thesis repository).

## Are there open-source GitHub repos, Kaggle notebooks, or detailed blog posts (including EPAT project reports) describing a specific systematic Nifty/Bank Nifty option-selling algorithm with mechanical entry/exit/regime-filter detail comparable to a per-regime LightGBM win-probability model?

### Takeaway
Nothing at comparable mechanical depth to a per-regime ML entry-timing model was found. GitHub has scattered utility repos (option-chain fetchers, strike-selection helpers, a simple time-based straddle script) but no repo combining VIX-regime routing with an ML win-probability classifier for Nifty/Bank Nifty option selling. No specific EPAT capstone report on Nifty option selling was located by title; EPAT's own published-project showcase exists but the specific project (if any) was not surfaced by these queries. One ML-for-short-straddle academic framing exists, but it is for the **S&P 500**, not an Indian index.

### Cited Findings
- **RustAlgo/920_Straddle** (GitHub, surfaced via ecosyste.ms topic listing): described as a "Time Based Straddle (9:20)" strategy — i.e., a simple rule-based intraday short straddle entered at a fixed clock time, not ML-driven, and with no VIX-regime routing described in the listing — [awesome.ecosyste.ms nifty50 topic page](https://awesome.ecosyste.ms/projects/github.com%2Fnix1%2Fbye) / [GitHub topics: nifty50](https://github.com/topics/nifty50)
- **srikar-kodakandla/fully-automated-nifty-options-trading** (GitHub): automates Nifty options trading via Zerodha Kite (using Selenium against the website rather than the paid API); tagged backtesting + options-trading, but the description gives no entry-logic or regime-filter detail beyond "it trades for you in Nifty options" — [GitHub topics: nifty50](https://github.com/topics/nifty50)
- **TechfaneTechnologies/QtsApp** and related repos (option-chain-notifier, option-chain-refresher, ZerodhaStrikesAllowedToTrade): all are data-plumbing utilities (option-chain fetch, Greeks/IV display, strike-availability lookup) — useful as infrastructure references but contain no entry/exit strategy logic — [GitHub topics: nifty50](https://github.com/topics/nifty50)
- **Nubra backtest engine** (PyPI package, not a GitHub repo per se): a day-by-day backtesting engine for NSE F&O options strategies with "pluggable strike selection, delta-drift rebalancing, and profit-target/stop-loss exits," backed by a DuckDB cache over the Nubra API — this is a generic *backtesting framework* for Indian options, not a published strategy with specific rules or results, and it requires proprietary Nubra API access — [PyPI: nubra-backtest-engine](https://pypi.org/project/nubra-backtest-engine/)
- **ML-for-short-straddle academic framing (non-India comparator):** a working paper frames deciding whether to execute a daily short straddle on the **S&P 500** as a supervised binary-classification problem; initial results showed no statistically significant outperformance vs. a "trade always" baseline, though a later gradient-boosted-tree model reportedly did show a statistically significant benefit over that baseline — this is the closest methodological analogue found to the user's per-regime LightGBM win-probability gate, but it is US-index, not Nifty/Bank Nifty — [The AI Finance Frontier: "Machine Learning Short Straddles — S&P 500"](https://theaifinancefrontier.beehiiv.com/p/machine-learning-short-straddles-sp500)
- **QuantInsti EPAT projects generally**: QuantInsti's own article describes EPAT capstone projects as strategies "built, tested, and documented using real market data," with many selected for publication on the QuantInsti blog — confirming the publication channel exists — but no specific Nifty-option-selling capstone title or report was found by these queries, including a direct `blog.quantinsti.com` + "Nifty option selling" search which returned no QuantInsti-hosted results at all — [QuantInsti: "What is an EPAT Project?"](https://www.quantinsti.com/articles/algo-trading-projects/)
- An EPAT-related Bank Nifty project that *was* found used TensorFlow/Keras/XGBoost/SMOTE to **predict Bank Nifty open prices** — a price-direction-prediction project, not an options-selling strategy — [QuantInsti blog: EPAT project, price prediction with deep learning (Bala Murugan Ganeshan)](https://www.quantinsti.com/articles/epat-project-price-prediction-deep-learning-bala-murugan-ganeshan/)
- One unverified, self-reported claim via a LinkedIn profile snippet (surfaced in search, not independently fetched) describes a Nifty short-premium strategy built from NSE bhavcopy data from February 2019, claiming a 2.06 Sharpe and 4.3% max drawdown net of STT/exchange charges/slippage, with parameters frozen on an in-sample half and tested out-of-sample. This is a single unverified secondary mention with no accessible primary report, blog post, or repo link — flagged here only as a lead, not a finding, since it cannot be independently verified or cited to a primary source within this pass.

### Inferences
- The gap between abundant *infrastructure* repos (option-chain fetchers, strike pickers) and near-absent *strategy* repos with disclosed mechanics is consistent with option-selling edge being either (a) kept private by the people who find it, or (b) genuinely hard to productize as a repo because it depends on live broker API access, proprietary option-chain history, or both — barriers this codebase's own `chain_engine.py`/NSE-archive-licensing gotcha illustrates independently (archive data is "licence-restricted internal data: never commit or redistribute").

## Is there coverage of "quant options selling" / "systematic options income" as a growing category among Indian HNI/family-office investors or fintech-native quant funds (smallcase-style baskets, SEBI-registered algo advisors)?

### Takeaway
There is active regulatory and media attention on algo-trading growth and retail options-selling risk in India generally, and on specific fintech-native quant asset managers (AlphaGrep, True Beacon, Quantace) moving into retail/HNI products — but no source in this pass identifies a named "systematic options income" product category, smallcase basket, or SEBI-registered-advisor offering built specifically around index-options-selling as its stated strategy.

### Cited Findings
- SEBI's retail-algo framework (effective **April 1, 2026** per one source) formally brings third-party algo-strategy providers under a broker-as-principal / algo-provider-as-agent structure, each algo needing exchange permission — a structural change that would apply to any options-selling algo sold to retail, but is not options-specific — [AlgoTest: "Algo Trading India: Complete Guide for Retail Traders (2026)"](https://algotest.in/blog/algo-trading-india.md)
- Per the same source, providers of "black-box" algo strategies (hidden logic) must be registered as a SEBI Research Analyst with documented research reports for each strategy — directly relevant to any firm wanting to publicly sell an options-selling signal service, but describes the regulatory requirement, not an existing named product — [AlgoTest: "Algo Trading India"](https://algotest.in/blog/algo-trading-india.md)
- A Moneylife opinion piece argues that unregistered options/futures advice distributed via algos violates SEBI's investment-advisory rules — an opinion/commentary piece highlighting the "illegal algo advisory" problem as a live concern, not a confirmed enforcement outcome — [Moneylife: "Why Is 'Illegal' Investment Advisory Business Thriving"](https://moneylife.in/article/why-is-illegal-investment-advisory-business-thriving-and-what-is-the-way-out/68335.html)
- **Quantace** runs several smallcase-style baskets (small-cap, manufacturing, mobility) that rebalance monthly/weekly and, per their own description, may shift into "liquid, gold, and silver instruments during volatile periods" as a hedging mechanism — these are equity/multi-asset rotation baskets, not options-selling-income products — [smallcase: Quantace India Manufacturing](https://www.smallcase.com/smallcase/quantace-india-manufacturing-QURENM_0003); [smallcase: Quantace Mobility](https://www.smallcase.com/smallcase/quantace-mobility-QURENM_0006)
- AlphaGrep's and True Beacon's moves into retail/HNI-facing products (mutual fund and AIF respectively — see firm-level section above) confirm the broader trend of India's quant prop talent pool moving toward asset-management products for retail/HNI/family-office money, but neither is marketed as an "options income" product per the sources found.
- Macro/regulatory framing: SEBI's October 2024 curbs on weekly index-options speculation, and the $7B FY24 algo/prop profit figure (see firm-level section), together support the narrative that systematic/algo participation in Indian index options has grown large enough to draw sustained regulatory attention — but this is framed as a financial-stability concern about speculative retail/algo volume, not as coverage of a specific "systematic options income" investor category — [BNN Bloomberg, Oct 2024](https://bnnbloomberg.ca/business/international/2024/10/03/why-indias-giant-options-market-poses-a-danger-to-financial-stability)

### Inferences
- The absence of a named "quant options income" smallcase or retail product, despite (a) SEBI's new regulatory scaffolding for exactly this kind of algo-advisory product and (b) heavy retail/speculative options-selling activity on NSE generally, suggests this category — if it exists in any formal form — is either very new (post-dating most indexed coverage), operating informally/unregistered (which the Moneylife piece flags as a live regulatory risk), or marketed under generic "algo trading" / "options trading education" branding rather than an "income" or "quant basket" label that these queries would catch.
- Family-office/HNI exposure to systematic options strategies in India appears to run through AIF structures (True Beacon-style) rather than smallcase-style retail baskets, likely because SEBI's F&O exposure limits for retail/smallcase-type vehicles are more restrictive than for AIFs — consistent with an unverified forum claim (TradingQnA) that AIFs face specific SEBI F&O exposure caps, though that claim itself was not independently confirmed in this pass.

### Gaps
- No source in this pass named a specific SEBI-registered Research Analyst or Investment Adviser whose disclosed strategy is systematic Nifty/Bank Nifty option selling (as opposed to general "algo trading" education/signal services). A targeted search on SEBI's own RA/RIA registry combined with "options" would be a logical next step but was outside this pass's scope.
- No press coverage (Economic Times / Moneycontrol / Business Standard feature-length profile) specifically on "systematic options selling as an HNI asset class" was found; the Business Standard and dailyexcelsior.com results found were about True Beacon's general fund structure, not a trend piece on the category itself.
- Could not confirm current (2026) status of SEBI's weekly-expiry options curbs or the April 2026 retail-algo framework beyond the secondary summaries in AlgoTest's blog; primary SEBI circular text was not fetched in this pass.
