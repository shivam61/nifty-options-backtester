## Streak (streak.tech): What published strategy templates exist for Nifty/Bank Nifty options selling, and what entry/strike/exit/SL rules are specified?

### Takeaway
No official Streak.tech blog page or help-center rule-set was directly retrievable in this session; what is documented comes from Streak users on Zerodha's TradingQnA forum reproducing "the blog's" rules for a Nifty/Bank Nifty short-straddle template, plus one Streak-affiliated forum reply on backtest limitations. The reproduced template is a classic 9:20 AM ATM short straddle with a 40% per-leg premium stop-loss and no fixed profit target.

### Cited Findings
- A TradingQnA user describes a "Nifty Income short straddle" strategy built on Streak, "as per the blog," with: **Entry Time: 9:20, Monday to Thursday; Exit Time: 3:15; Stop-Loss: 40% of each leg's premium**; no fixed target was mentioned — [TradingQnA: Is "Nifty Income short straddle" strategy on streak really profitable?](https://tradingqna.com/t/is-nifty-income-short-straddle-strategy-on-streak-really-profitable-see-details/122634)
- A separate TradingQnA thread on a BankNifty straddle template built on Streak's "dynamic contract" feature used a **1 PM entry**, exit at **3 PM or on stop-loss**, with SL/target left user-configurable (described as "randomly selected" defaults the user can change) — [TradingQnA: Bank Nifty Straddle Backtest in Streak](https://tradingqna.com/t/bank-nifty-straddle-backtest-in-streak/136147)
- A Streak-affiliated forum responder states explicitly that **Streak's backtest does not account for margin**, and recommends evaluating strategies on absolute cumulative P&L rather than percentage return, after also factoring in brokerage, taxes, and slippage — [TradingQnA thread (same as above)](https://tradingqna.com/t/is-nifty-income-short-straddle-strategy-on-streak-really-profitable-see-details/122634)
- A Streak user reports an execution-timing issue: after a stop-loss or target is hit, the platform's next sell order (e.g., in a straddle with re-entry) takes about a **1-minute gap** to execute, which is an operational/slippage risk for mechanical SL/re-entry logic — [TradingQnA: Streak scanner thread](https://tradingqna.com/t/streak-scanner-for-nifty-intraday/136112/2)
- Other Streak forum threads exist discussing "Short Straddle with Adjustment" and "Time Based Iron Condor in Streak," confirming Streak supports templated straddle/iron-condor construction with adjustment logic, but the threads' rule content was not retrievable via search snippet in this session — [TradingQnA: Short Straddle with Adjustment](https://tradingqna.com/t/short-straddle-with-adjustment-streak-strategy-help-needed/149668); [TradingQnA: Time Based Iron Condor in Streak](https://tradingqna.com/t/time-based-iron-condor-in-streak/138414)

### Inferences
- Streak's own marketing/help material appears to function as a no-code "scan-condition → strategy" builder (it evaluates entry/exit conditions on closed OHLC candles, triggering on the next candle's open) rather than publishing a single canonical "recommended" options-selling rule set; the concrete numeric rules in circulation (9:20 entry, 40% SL, 3:15 exit) seem to originate from a Streak blog post that users then cloned, rather than being a current, prominently-featured official template.
- Because Streak's backtest explicitly ignores margin, any self-reported backtest P&L/return percentages for Streak straddle templates should be treated as unreliable for capital-efficiency conclusions, per Streak's own forum acknowledgment.

## AlgoTest (algotest.in): What backtested option-selling templates/blog posts describe specific rule sets? What entry timing, strike selection, position sizing, and SL/adjustment rules are published?

### Takeaway
AlgoTest's own blog content retrieved via search summary is conceptual/educational (e.g., explaining straddle payoff structure) rather than prescribing one canonical numeric rule set like "sell ATM straddle at 9:20, SL 30%, target 50%." However, AlgoTest's backtesting *platform* (as distinct from a specific published strategy) does expose a documented, generic rule framework — entry/exit time fields, partial vs. complete square-off, break-even trailing, and multiple re-entry modes — that users fill in with their own numbers rather than AlgoTest publishing one "recommended" parameter set.

### Cited Findings
- AlgoTest's "Short Straddle Strategy" blog post (title: "Short Straddle Strategy: Payoff, Breakeven, Risks and Nifty Example") describes the structure as selling an ATM call and put at the same strike/expiry and explains that a large index move can produce losses exceeding the premium collected, but the retrieved summary contains no specific entry clock-time, SL%, or target% — [AlgoTest blog: Short Straddle Strategy](https://algotest.in/blog/short-straddle-strategy/)
- AlgoTest's "Key Terms Related to Algo Trading" and strategy-builder documentation describe the backtest workflow as: pick strategy type (Intraday / BTST / Positional) → choose index/stock and cash-vs-futures basis for strikes → build legs → add stop-loss, target, and re-entry rules → set date range → run backtest — [AlgoTest: Key Terms](https://algotest.in/blog/definition-of-terms-used-in-backtesting-platform/); [AlgoTest: Optimize Your Selling with Option Strategy Builder](https://algotest.in/blog/optimize-sales-with-algotest-option-strategy-builder/)
- For intraday strategies, the platform requires an **entry time** and a forced **exit time** (end-of-day square-off), plus an optional cutoff after which no fresh entries are taken — [AlgoTest: Key Terms](https://algotest.in/blog/definition-of-terms-used-in-backtesting-platform/)
- Stop-loss/target behavior is configurable as **partial square-off** (SL/target hit on one leg closes only that leg) or **complete square-off** (a hit on any leg closes all legs), plus an optional **break-even trail**: once one leg is stopped out, the stop-loss on the remaining legs is moved to their own entry price — [AlgoTest: Key Terms](https://algotest.in/blog/definition-of-terms-used-in-backtesting-platform/)
- Re-entry options include re-entering at original cost (which can flip into an opposite/long position) and **momentum-based re-entry** — the cited worked example is "Simple Momentum = Percentage Up 10%, buy ATM call, entry time 9:30 AM, SL 5%" — i.e., the platform waits for a defined adverse move before re-entering — [AlgoTest: Key Terms](https://algotest.in/blog/definition-of-terms-used-in-backtesting-platform/)
- AlgoTest allows users to add slippage and brokerage assumptions to backtests and offers a forward-testing (paper-trading on live data) mode before going live — [AlgoTest: Key Terms](https://algotest.in/blog/definition-of-terms-used-in-backtesting-platform/)

### Inferences
- AlgoTest functions primarily as a flexible backtesting/strategy-builder *engine* for option sellers to test their own numeric assumptions, not as a publisher of one fixed "sell ATM straddle at X time with Y% SL" template the way a single blog post might. The oft-quoted "sell ATM straddle at 9:20 AM, SL 30%, target 50%" style of rule (referenced in the task prompt) appears to be a widely-circulated *generic* Indian retail-options-selling heuristic rather than a specific AlgoTest-published number — it shows up (with varying percentages: 35-40%) attributed instead to Marketcalls.in and to anonymous forum templates (see below).
- AlgoTest's blog is comparison/educational-content-heavy (e.g., "AlgoTest vs Quantsapp," "AlgoTest vs Dhan," "Best Option Strategy Builders") rather than a strategy-rules library in the sense QuantInsti's blog is for global systematic strategies.

### Gaps
- Could not directly fetch algotest.in pages (DNS resolution failure via the available web-fetch tool for this domain in this session), so full text of AlgoTest's "Short Straddle Strategy," "Iron Condor Strategy," and "Option Strategy Builder" posts could not be confirmed beyond search-engine summaries. Any numeric SL/target/strike-selection defaults actually printed in those posts (if any) are unconfirmed.
- No AlgoTest-published backtest performance statistics (win rate, average P&L, drawdown) for any named option-selling template were found; AlgoTest's blog content retrieved is explanatory/comparative, not a performance report.

## Sensibull (sensibull.com): What strategy-builder recommended setups / "options strategies" guides exist for Nifty selling, and what adjustment logic is published?

### Takeaway
No Sensibull-authored blog post on iron-condor/straddle adjustment logic was directly retrieved; what's available is third-party description of Sensibull's Strategy Builder mechanics (template auto-fill behavior) plus generic (non-Sensibull-specific) options-adjustment heuristics that several secondary sources attribute to "common practice" rather than to a Sensibull rule page.

### Cited Findings
- A secondhand trader description of Sensibull's workflow: select Nifty and an expiry in Strategy Builder, then use the "Popular Strategies" → "Iron Condor" template, which auto-fills legs based on the current Nifty price; one cited default example sells strikes ~200 points from ATM and buys hedges ~400 points out — [Multibagg.ai: sensibull opstra four leg execution](https://www.multibagg.ai/market-pulse/articles/sensibull-opstra-four-leg-execution-cms5e9xim00000znznr4wspak) (secondhand summary, not Sensibull's own page — flagged as unverified in the app)
- A 2023 TradingView write-up using Sensibull to build a four-leg Nifty weekly iron condor reports a specific worked trade: **max profit ≈ ₹2,468, max loss ≈ ₹5,033** on that particular setup — a single anecdotal example, not a general rule — [TradingView: Firefighting a Short Iron Condor Strategy on Nifty50 on Expiry Day](https://es.tradingview.com/chart/NIFTY/2tjtkZAv-Firefighting-a-Short-Iron-Condor-Strategy-on-Nifty50-on-Expiry-D)
- General (non-Sensibull-specific) adjustment heuristics repeated across secondary sources discussing iron condors on Nifty: (1) **roll the tested side** further out-of-the-money when threatened (costs money, buys room); (2) **roll the untested side** inward to collect more premium and offset cost (increases risk on that side); (3) **convert to a directional spread** by closing the threatened side and keeping the safe side if a real breakout is underway; (4) **delta-hedge** with the underlying/futures near expiry — [Barchart: How to Adjust Iron Condors When Tested](https://www.barchart.com/story/news/29998131/how-to-adjust-iron-condors-when-tested); [5paisa: Iron Condor in a Directional Market](https://www.5paisa.com/stock-market-guide/derivatives-trading-basics/iron-condor-in-directional-market)
- A trader forum rule-of-thumb (not vetted, not platform-published): roll the untested side once the tested side's premium roughly **doubles**; after multiple adjustments, closing the trade is often preferable to further rolling — [TradingQnA: How do you adjust your iron condors?](https://tradingqna.com/t/how-do-you-adjust-your-iron-condors-what-are-the-trigger-points-for-adjustments/108139)
- A general (non-Sensibull) Nifty iron-condor exit rule cited by one aggregator: **exit at 50-75% of maximum profit, or when loss reaches 2x the initial credit received** — [secondary aggregator source, exact attribution unclear from snippet]
- Neostox's iron-condor guide advises setting the adjustment rule *before* entry, and cautions that judging an adjustment purely by whether spot has touched the short strike is incomplete — remaining premium, days-to-expiry, and IV also matter — [Neostox: Iron Condor Strategy in Options Trading](https://blog.neostox.com/options-strategies/iron-condor-strategy-in-options-trading)

### Inferences
- Sensibull's public-facing role, based on available evidence, is predominantly as a **strategy construction/visualization and OI-analytics tool** (payoff charts, Greeks, option chain, template auto-fill) rather than a publisher of a specific, numbered "roll when X, convert when Y" adjustment rulebook; the mechanical adjustment rules circulating online and cited above are generic options-trading practice echoed by many sites, not uniquely sourced to Sensibull's own documentation.
- The "sell ~200 pts from ATM / hedge ~400 pts out" default attributed to Sensibull's Iron Condor template, if accurate, would be a concrete, citable strike-selection rule — but it is only secondhand-sourced here and should be treated as unverified pending direct confirmation from Sensibull's own help center.

## Other platforms (uTrade Algos, TradeSmart, Marketcalls.in, QuantInsti): concrete rule-based Nifty/Bank Nifty weekly or monthly option-selling systems with parameters

### Takeaway
Marketcalls.in is the most concretely rule-documented source found: it publishes the well-known "9:20 straddle" with explicit numeric entry time, strike method (ATM), stop-loss percentage, and exit time, across multiple articles (with minor variant numbers), and candidly reports that the edge has decayed since 2023. uTrade Algos publishes named strategies ("Brahmastra Nifty," "Chakravyuh Ka Tod Nifty") with VIX-range guidance and occasional specific SL/target rupee values in its newsletters, but not a full transparent rule sheet. TradeSmart and QuantInsti yielded no directly-sourced, platform-published mechanical rule sets in this session — a clear gap for both.

### Cited Findings
**Marketcalls.in — "9:20 straddle" (most concrete rule set found in this research):**
- Bank Nifty variant: **sell ATM CE and ATM PE weekly options at 9:20 AM** ("Short Bank Nifty ATM CE and ATM PE Weekly Options (Short Straddle) at 9.20 a.m."); once filled, **place a 35% stop-loss order on both legs**; if one leg's SL triggers, the other leg is left open unless/until its own SL triggers — [Marketcalls.in, via search summary](https://www.marketcalls.in/futures-and-options/how-the-9-20-intraday-straddlers-are-being-gamed.html)
- Nifty variant: **sell the Nifty straddle (ATM CE + ATM PE) exactly at 9:20 AM**, with a **fixed 40% stop-loss on both legs using SL-M (stop-loss market) orders**; **exit the straddle at 3:10 PM** if neither SL is hit; if one leg's SL hits, continue holding the other leg with its own stop-loss — [Marketcalls.in, via search summary](https://www.marketcalls.in/futures-and-options/is-the-920-straddle-no-more-working.html)
- Marketcalls explicitly flags an execution-risk caveat: **SL-M orders are vulnerable to "stop hunting"/poor fills** in thin/volatile conditions, especially for far-OTM strikes, producing "extraordinary slippage" — [Marketcalls.in, via search summary](https://www.marketcalls.in/futures-and-options/is-the-920-straddle-no-more-working.html)
- Marketcalls reports (self-assessed, not independently audited) that the **9:20 straddle's edge has faded, especially in 2023**, and suggests that **shifting entry to 10:30 AM** could have performed meaningfully better than the classic 9:20 entry in recent years — [Marketcalls.in: Is the 920 straddle no more working](https://www.marketcalls.in/futures-and-options/is-the-920-straddle-no-more-working.html)
- Corroborating (non-Marketcalls) forum description of the same template: short ATM call and put at 9:20 with a fixed stop of roughly **25-35%**, exiting the stopped leg while holding the other to end of day — [TradingQnA: Detailed analysis of 920 Short Straddle Strategy](https://tradingqna.com/t/detailed-analysis-of-920-short-straddle-strategy-is-it-stopped-working/130677) / [ATM Short Straddle Intraday Strategy - Nifty & Bank Nifty](https://tradingqna.com/t/atm-short-straddle-intraday-strategy-nifty-bank-nifty/88032)
- ICICIdirect also republishes/discusses the "9:20 AM short straddle" as a named retail intraday strategy, confirming it is a widely recognized template across Indian broker/content sites, not unique to Marketcalls — [ICICIdirect: All about 9:20 AM Short Straddle](https://www.icicidirect.com/futures-and-options/articles/all-about-9-20-am-short-straddle-a-powerful-intraday-trading-strategy)

**uTrade Algos:**
- "Brahmastra Nifty" and "Chakravyuh Ka Tod Nifty" are named, packaged Nifty option-selling algo products with **recommended India VIX operating ranges disclosed**: Brahmastra Nifty is listed with a recommended VIX range of **15-30**; Chakravyuh Ka Tod Nifty with **12-30** — [uTrade Algos product pages, via search summary](https://www.utradealgos.com/brahmastra-nifty)
