# SEBI-Registered PMS/AIF Vehicles Running Nifty/Bank Nifty Options-Selling or "Options Income" Strategies

## Which SEBI-registered PMS providers or AIFs market "options income" / "low volatility" / "iron condor" / "covered call" strategies on Nifty/Bank Nifty index options?

### Takeaway
No PMS or AIF surfaced in this research that markets a *dedicated, named* Nifty/Bank Nifty **index options-selling income product** with a public methodology write-up comparable to a retail "iron condor" or "short strangle" strategy page. What does exist, and is SEBI-registered, falls into three different buckets that are easy to conflate: (1) Category III AIF **long-short/market-neutral equity** funds that use index & stock F&O as a tool within a broader equity book (Nuvama EDGE, Avendus Market Neutral Fund, Avendus Absolute Return Fund); (2) a handful of named firms often associated with "options income" in investor forums/press (Capitalmind, True Beacon, Alpha Alternatives, Right Horizons, Weekend Investing, Quantace, Abans, Tamohara, Pace360) whose *current, official* product lineups — as far as this search could verify — are predominantly equity/multi-asset PMS or AIF strategies, not standalone options-selling products; and (3) a SEBI-registered research-analyst/investment-adviser model-portfolio product (Wright Research's "Theta Decay Intraday") that explicitly sells weekly-expiry options for theta decay, but at a ₹2 lakh minimum ticket — well below the ₹50 lakh SEBI PMS minimum — meaning it is very unlikely to be a SEBI PMS Regulations product and more likely a smallcase-style RA/IA model portfolio.

### Inferences
- Within India's regulated PMS/AIF universe, "options income"-style strategies appear to live mostly as a *sub-component inside long-short or hedging mandates* (Nuvama EDGE, Avendus funds, Right Horizons' "tactical hedging") rather than as standalone, prominently-marketed products — in contrast to the heavy retail/influencer marketing of "options selling," "theta," and "iron condor" strategies outside the PMS/AIF wrapper (educational content, broker blogs, smallcase-style RA/IA portfolios like Wright Research).
- Capitalmind's historical "Uncle Theta"/"StratOptions" content is the closest match in the search results to a named, Nifty-options-selling, income-generation strategy associated with a SEBI-registered PMS brand, but its current status (whether still offered, and under what registration) could not be confirmed and should not be assumed current.
- The scarcity of dedicated, clearly-labeled options-income PMS/AIF products — combined with the SEBI AUM data below showing equity derivatives as a tiny sliver of total PMS AUM — suggests pure index-options-selling is a niche strategy within India's regulated discretionary-management industry, likely because of SEBI's derivative exposure limits for PMS (generally restricted to hedging/rebalancing, not directional/income-generating net short options exposure) and the ₹50 lakh+ (PMS) / ₹1 crore (AIF) minimum ticket sizes that keep this segment small and institutionally thin.

Disclosed methodology detail is thin and inconsistent across sources; no fund in this research disclosed Nifty/Bank Nifty options-selling mechanics (days-to-expiry preference, strike selection, hedging ratio, specific stop-loss/drawdown triggers) at the level of specificity the backtester project tracks for its own strategies. ### Inferences
- The absence of disclosed DTE preferences, strike-selection rules, or numeric stop-loss/max-drawdown triggers across every fund found is consistent with SEBI PMS/AIF disclosure norms, which require strategy-objective-level and risk-category disclosure in the Schedule V disclosure document / PPM, but do not require (and these managers evidently do not volunteer) the granular mechanical rules that a systematic options-selling backtester would need to replicate a strategy.
- The SEBI 2024 weekly-expiry consolidation (Bank Nifty/FinNifty/Midcap Nifty discontinued) is a structural, dated, verifiable regulatory event that would have forced any India-based systematic options-selling PMS/AIF using those indices to re-engineer its approach around Nifty 50 weekly/monthly contracts from November 2024 onward — this is a hard constraint worth noting for any comparison with the backtester's own Nifty-only, Thursday/Tuesday-expiry mechanics.

### Gaps
- No fund's disclosure document, PPM, or factsheet was directly read (only secondary/press descriptions) in this session; numeric risk-control parameters (stop-loss %, max drawdown limit, margin/leverage ratio actually used, DTE entry/exit rules) were not found for any fund.
- Could not confirm whether the SEBI "new asset class between MF and PMS" proposal (2024) was finalized, and if so, under what final derivative limits — this matters because it is the regulatory lane most likely to eventually host a retail-accessible, higher-derivative-exposure "options income" product in India.
- Could not verify the primary SEBI circular text on PMS derivative-exposure limits (only a forum paraphrase was found); a direct read of the relevant SEBI (Portfolio Managers) Regulations, 2020 and any amendment circulars would be needed to state the exact permitted derivative exposure/purpose rules with confidence.

## Is there any audited or regulator-filed performance data (SEBI PMS disclosure documents, APMI aggregated data, factsheets) showing actual returns/volatility/drawdown for an options-selling PMS/AIF specifically?

### Takeaway
Aggregated regulator-sourced data (SEBI's monthly PMS AUM statistics) indicates that "equity derivatives" is a vanishingly small category of total PMS assets in India, and fund-level CAGR data exists (via PMS Bazaar, which aggregates SEBI-mandated PMS performance disclosures) for long-short funds like Nuvama EDGE and for Capitalmind's current equity PMS approaches — but no performance series specifically isolates a pure options-selling/premium-harvesting mandate, and no volatility or max-drawdown figures (as opposed to headline CAGR) were found for any such fund.

### Inferences
- The SEBI-reported near-zero scale of "equity derivatives" AUM within the PMS industry (as distinct from AIFs, which are a separate, generally larger and more leverage-permissive regulatory category) strongly implies that no meaningfully-sized, SEBI-registered discretionary PMS in India currently runs a large, pure index-options-selling book under the PMS wrapper — to the extent options-selling/premium-harvesting happens at scale in regulated vehicles, it is more likely housed in Category III AIFs (higher leverage ceiling, HNI/institutional-only, ₹1 crore+ minimum, PPM-based disclosure rather than the more retail-facing PMS disclosure document) — though this research found no AIF-level aggregate breakout isolating options-selling exposure either.
- Headline CAGR figures reported by aggregators like PMS Bazaar for blended long-short funds (Nuvama EDGE, Capitalmind Surge India) cannot be used as a proxy for "options-selling strategy performance" because the options/derivatives component is one input among several (cash equities, long-short equity, hedging) within each fund.

### Gaps
- No volatility (standard deviation) or maximum-drawdown figure was found for any named fund in this research — all performance data located was limited to headline CAGR/trailing-return figures.
- Could not directly access or verify SEBI's April 2025 PMS derivative-exposure figures, the DSP Cat III AIF benchmark report, or the Preqin India AIF benchmark — all three would likely contain the most relevant regulator-adjacent or industry-aggregated performance/risk data and should be prioritized for direct retrieval in any follow-up research (note: DNS-level WebFetch failures to pmsbazaar.com and dspim.com in this session suggest a possible network/proxy restriction rather than the content being unavailable — worth retrying with a different fetch method or at a later time).
- No source found disaggregates APMI/SEBI PMS or AIF performance data by *strategy type* (e.g., "derivatives-based" or "options-income" as a labeled category with its own return/volatility series) — SEBI and APMI publish AUM by instrument-exposure category, not performance by strategy-label.
- Could not confirm whether any AIF files audited, strategy-specific performance (as opposed to AUM) with SEBI at all, or whether that is only disclosed privately to AIF unit-holders via the PPM/private placement route (which is not publicly searchable).

## Are there any documented failures, SEBI actions, or investor complaints against PMS/AIF options-selling schemes in India?

### Takeaway
This research found several SEBI enforcement actions and investor-complaint patterns against PMS/AIF providers in India (unregistered PMS operation, AIF governance/PPM-deviation lapses, mis-selling complaint-handling failures), but **none of the SEBI orders or press coverage found names an options-selling or options-income strategy specifically as the cause of investor loss or regulatory action** — the violations found are governance, registration, and process failures, not strategy-specific blow-ups parallel to global options-selling fund collapses (e.g., LJM, Catalyst/James Cordier-type events in the US).

### Inferences
- The absence of any identified options-selling-specific SEBI action or investor-complaint case is itself informative given (a) the scale of retail/influencer attention to options-selling strategies in India generally and (b) the global precedent for such strategies causing dramatic, well-publicized fund losses — it suggests either that regulated (PMS/AIF) options-selling exposure in India is too small in scale to have produced a headline-grabbing blow-up (consistent with the near-zero "equity derivatives" AUM figure above), or that any such losses have occurred within funds' normal return variance without triggering a distinct SEBI enforcement action, or both.
- Most of the enforcement/complaint activity found (unregistered PMS operation, AIF PPM/governance deviations, distributor-framework gaps) reflects a broader pattern of registration and process risk in India's PMS/AIF industry rather than product/strategy risk specific to derivatives or options.

### Gaps

## Separately: is there any mutual fund (not PMS/AIF) in India running a buy-write/covered-call or options-income strategy?

### Takeaway
Yes, but only in a narrow, SEBI-constrained form: since a January 2019 SEBI circular, Indian equity mutual fund schemes (excluding index funds/ETFs) are permitted to write **covered calls on individual stocks** that are constituents of benchmark indices (Nifty 50 / Sensex) and that the scheme already holds — this is stock-level buy-write, not an index-level Nifty/Bank Nifty options-selling strategy, and several AMCs have recently (2025) added this as an enabling provision in new or existing schemes. No Indian mutual fund scheme was found running a Nifty/Bank Nifty index-level covered-call or options-income strategy as its primary mandate.

### Cited Findings
- SEBI's January 2019 framework permits equity mutual fund schemes to write call options only on individual stocks that are constituents of benchmark indices (Nifty 50/Sensex); index funds and ETFs are excluded from this permission. — [Business Standard — SEBI allows mutual funds to write call options under certain conditions](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/article/markets/sebi-allows-mutual-funds-to-write-call-options-under-certain-conditions-119011700021_1.html); [SEBI circular, Jan 2019](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/attachdocs/jan-2019/1547638949673.pdf)
- Total notional value of covered calls written by a scheme is capped at 15% of the total market value of equity shares held by that scheme; and the shares underlying written calls cannot exceed 30% of the scheme's unencumbered holding in that particular stock. A scheme cannot write a call without holding the underlying shares (true "covered" call only — no naked index writing). Written calls are marked to market daily; a passive breach of limits gives the scheme 7 trading days to rebalance. — [Business Standard — SEBI allows mutual funds to write call options](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/article/markets/sebi-allows-mutual-funds-to-write-call-options-under-certain-conditions-119011700021_1.html)
- Business Standard reported (November 2025) that new mutual fund schemes with an enabling provision to use covered calls include the SBI Business Cycle Fund, Mirae Asset Infrastructure Fund, Kotak Rural Opportunities Fund, and ICICI Prudential Conglomerate Fund; and that an existing scheme, Mirae Asset Aggressive Hybrid Fund, notified investors it would modify the fund to add covered-call usage. One fund house characterized the approach as "a strategic tool to generate income rather than a response to short-term market conditions." — [Business Standard — MFs turn to covered call strategy to squeeze out gains in flat market](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/markets/mutual-fund/mfs-turn-to-covered-call-strategy-to-squeeze-out-gains-in-flat-market-125110702109_1.html)
- None of the schemes named above are index-options-selling or Nifty/Bank-Nifty-index covered-call funds — they are equity schemes that may, within the above limits, write calls on the individual stocks they hold.
- SEBI first restricted and then (Jan 2019) re-permitted covered call writing for mutual funds — an earlier 2010 restriction had disallowed it, which the 2019 circular lifted under the new conditions. — [search snippet summarizing Business Standard's Sebi-mulls coverage, Aug 2018](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/article/markets/sebi-mulls-use-of-covered-calls-for-mutual-funds-as-part-of-equity-strategy-118081800030_1.html)

### Inferences
- There is a clear regulatory distinction (relevant to the backtester's own domain) between what Indian mutual funds are permitted to do (single-stock covered calls, capped notional, must hold the underlying) and what the backtester / PMS-AIF options-selling landscape being researched is about (Nifty/Bank Nifty **index**-level options selling for income) — SEBI's mutual fund framework does not permit index-level covered call writing or any form of net-short index options overlay as a yield-enhancement strategy; that activity, to the extent it exists in a regulated wrapper at all in India, would have to sit in a Category III AIF (with its higher leverage ceiling and HNI-only access) rather than a retail mutual fund.
- The 2025 wave of AMCs adding covered-call enabling language (SBI, Mirae Asset, Kotak, ICICI Prudential) suggests growing mainstream adoption of the stock-level buy-write tool within flat/range-bound market conditions, but this is income-enhancement at the single-stock level within diversified equity funds, not a dedicated "options income fund" product category in India.

### Gaps
- Could not find any quantified performance data (income generated, impact on scheme NAV/returns) from any of the named schemes' actual use of the covered-call provision — only that the enabling provision exists/was recently added.
- Could not confirm whether any mutual fund scheme uses covered calls as its *primary* strategy (as opposed to an occasional income-enhancement tool within a broader equity mandate) — all examples found use it as a supplementary technique.
- Did not find evidence of any proposal or product (MF, or the SEBI-proposed "new asset class between MF and PMS") that would permit Nifty/Bank Nifty index-level options-selling for retail investors in a mutual-fund-like wrapper in India.
